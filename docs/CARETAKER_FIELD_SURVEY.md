# Caretaker Field Survey
**Date:** October 22, 2026
**Purpose:** Close the single biggest evidence gap — what a caretaker actually earns, and
what it would cost to convert him off the viewing-fee economy
**Depends on:** `HOUSE_HUNTING_SOCIAL_RESEARCH_KENYA.md`, `CARETAKER_AGENT_INCENTIVE_DESIGN.md`
**Mode:** Face-to-face, one-on-one, administered by a field researcher. **Not** a self-serve form.
**Length:** 20–25 minutes
**Target:** 15 completed interviews (5 per estate type)

## PART 1 — FIELD RESEARCHER BRIEFING

**Read this fully before your first interview. The survey will produce garbage if you skip it.**

### 1.1 The one thing that can ruin this research

If the caretaker thinks **saying "yes" gets him a job or money from us**, every answer in
Section G becomes worthless. He will tell you what he thinks you want to hear.

**You must neutralise this out loud, in the opening script, and again before Section G:**

> *"We are not hiring today. Nobody gets a job from this conversation. I just need the
> truth, even if the truth is that this idea is bad."*

Say it even if it feels awkward. Say it twice.

### 1.2 Rules

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Interview **one-on-one** | Interview with the landlord, another caretaker, or an agent present |
| Sit outside the plot if possible — a kibanda, a bench | Stand at the gate where tenants walk past |
| Use **paper**, write as he talks | Audio-record — it kills honesty on the money questions |
| Let silence sit. People fill it | Fill the silence yourself |
| Ask the **open** version first, then the options | Read the options first (it anchors him) |
| Accept "I don't know" and move on | Push for a number he doesn't have |
| Pay the thank-you **whether or not he finishes** | Make payment conditional on answers |
| Speak Swahili or Sheng if that's easier for him | Force English |

### 1.3 The money questions (Section E) — handle with care

Viewing fees are often taken **without the landlord's knowledge.** A direct "do you charge
people?" gets a lie. The section is built to work around this, in this exact order:

1. Ask about **other caretakers** first ("how do caretakers around here get extra?")
2. Ask what the **normal amount** is around here — not what *he* charges
3. Only at the end, ask about **him personally** — and let him skip it

**Never express surprise, judgement, or disapproval.** No "really?", no raised eyebrows, no
"isn't that illegal?". He is telling you how he feeds his family. If you flinch, the
interview is over even if he keeps talking.

### 1.4 Sampling

| Estate type | Examples | Target |
|---|---|---|
| High-density, low rent | Pipeline, Zimmerman, Kawangware, Githurai | 5 |
| Satellite town | Ruiru, Juja, Kitengela, Rongai | 5 |
| Mid / upper-mid | Kilimani-adjacent, South B/C, Ruaka | 5 |

Recruit by walking in and asking for the caretaker. Do not recruit through agents — they
will send you people who perform for them.

### 1.5 Thank-you payment

**KSh 300** airtime or M-Pesa, paid at the end, no conditions. Mention it in the consent so
he knows it exists, but make clear it is not payment for particular answers.

## PART 3 — WHAT WE DO WITH THE ANSWERS

### The five numbers that decide the business

| # | From | Number | Why it decides things |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **E1 + E11** | Salary + extra income | The income we must match or beat. Everything in the payment schedule is guesswork until we have this. |
| 2 | **G9** | Price to stop charging for viewings | The cost of converting one caretaker. Multiply by coverage target = our real CAC. |
| 3 | **C7** | Days a house sits empty | The landlord's loss, which funds the whole model. If this is already short, the subscription pitch weakens badly. |
| 4 | **C5** | Days of notice before a tenant leaves | Whether the 30-day early-warning engine is even possible. If tenants give 3 days, redesign. |
| 5 | **C4** | Tenants moving out per month | Inventory flow per building → how many buildings we need for a liquid market |

### Decision rules we commit to in advance

- **If G9 (conversion price) is routinely above KSh 8,000/month** → the caretaker-paid model
  does not pay for itself at KSh 50–100/unit subscription. Either raise the landlord price or
  change strategy to landlord-direct and treat caretakers as optional.
- **If C7 (void) is under 10 days** on average → the void-arbitrage pitch in the incentive
  doc is weak. Lead with tribunal-proof rent records instead (the §3.1 finding).
- **If E6 is mostly "the landlord does not know"** → landlord opt-in will expose caretakers
  and could get them fired. The design must then make the caretaker's participation
  invisible to the landlord, or we recruit landlords first and caretakers second.
- **If F1 shows mostly feature phones** → build USSD or a WhatsApp bot first, not an app.
- **If H3 shows real fear of retaliation** → anonymity is not a nice-to-have, it is a
  launch blocker.

### Sample size honesty
15 interviews gives **direction, not statistics.** It is enough to kill a wrong assumption
and enough to set a price range to test. It is not enough to claim "caretakers in Kenya
earn X." Write up findings as ranges and as counts out of 15 — never as percentages.

### Do not do these things with this data
- Do not record any caretaker's name next to his salary or fee answers in any shared file.
  Use interview codes (C01–C15). **Keep the name-to-code key separate and offline.**
- Do not show any landlord what their caretaker said. Ever. This was promised in the consent.
- Do not store ID numbers. We do not need them at this stage, and under the Data Protection
  Act 2019 collecting what we do not need is itself the risk.
