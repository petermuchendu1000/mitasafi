## 1. The Card's Job

A provider card is not a profile. It is a **decision compression surface**. Its sole purpose is to help a buyer answer one binary question: **"Should I click this or skip it?"**

Every element must serve exactly one of three functions:
1. **Eliminate** — Help the buyer dismiss irrelevant providers fast (location, price, availability)
2. **Differentiate** — Help the buyer choose between remaining candidates (rating, reviews, trust signals)
3. **Act** — Reduce friction to the next step (clear CTA, save for later)

If an element doesn't serve one of these, it's noise. Noise kills conversion.

### The 3-Second Rule

Eye-tracking studies on marketplace cards (Baymard Institute, NN Group) show:
- Users spend **2–4 seconds** on a card before deciding to click or scroll past
- They scan in an **F-pattern** (top-left → right → down-left)
- **Image → Price → Rating → Name** is the typical scan order on vertical cards
- More than 5–6 data points causes **cognitive overload** and decision paralysis

### Mitasafi's Context

This is a **high-consideration, trust-sensitive** marketplace. People are letting strangers into their homes. Unlike buying a $5 Fiverr gig, the stakes are:
- **Physical safety** (stranger in your house)
- **Property risk** (plumber could flood your bathroom)
- **Financial commitment** (KES 5,000–50,000+)
- **Time dependency** (emergency plumbing can't wait)
- **Repeat relationship** (cleaning, laundry = recurring)

This means trust signals carry *more* weight than on digital-service platforms. But they must be earned through substance, not decoration.

## 2. Platform Research

### Fiverr (Digital Services)

**Card shows:** Seller photo (circular), service image (wide), seller level badge, rating (star + number + count), title/description (2 lines), starting price, "I will..." tagline.

**What works:** The service image does the heavy lifting. Rating is prominent. Price is bottom-left — last thing scanned, anchors decision. Seller level badge (Level 1/2/Top Rated) is a single earned badge.

**What doesn't apply to us:** Fiverr cards are *service*-centric, not *seller*-centric. Each card is a specific gig. We're selling the *provider*, not a single service.

### Thumbtack (US Home Services)

**Card shows:** Provider photo, business name, rating (star + count), "X hires on Thumbtack", years in business, "Great value" / "Exceptional service" machine-generated badge, price estimate, "View profile" CTA.

**Key insight:** Thumbtack shows **"X hires on Thumbtack"** — platform-specific social proof that's more credible than self-reported "500 jobs completed". They also show machine-generated category badges ("Great value") derived from review analysis.

**What's brilliant:** The "X hires" number is verifiable, platform-native, and impossible to fake. Much stronger trust signal than generic review counts.

### TaskRabbit (Task-Based Services)

**Card shows:** Provider photo, first name + last initial, rating (star + count), # of tasks completed, "Elite Tasker" badge, hourly rate, "X% positive reviews", tag line.

**Key insight:** TaskRabbit uses **first name + last initial** (e.g., "John M.") — humanizes the card. Shows **completion count** and **positive review percentage** as separate signals. "Elite Tasker" is the only badge.

### Angi (Home Services, US)

**Card shows:** Business name, rating (star + count), years in business, license info, "Angi Certified" badge, lead form CTA (not a link — a button that opens a quote form).

**Key insight:** Angi leads with **years in business** and **license status** — trust signals that matter when someone's rewiring your house. The CTA is aggressive: it's a lead form, not a profile view.

### Bark (UK/Global Services)

**Card shows:** Business name, location, rating, "Hired X times on Bark", price range, response time ("Usually responds within 2 hours"), photos (carousel).

**Key insight:** **Response time as a card-level signal** is brilliant for service marketplaces. A plumber who responds in 20 minutes vs. 24 hours is a fundamentally different product.

### Google Local Services

**Card shows:** Business name, rating (star + count), years in business, "Google Guaranteed" badge, hours of operation, phone number.

**Key insight:** "Google Guaranteed" is the most trusted badge in local services because Google backs it with a money-back guarantee. The badge *means something*. Random "verified" or "pro" badges without substance are noise.

### Houzz (Home Design/Renovation)

**Card shows:** Portfolio image (large), business name, location, rating, "Best of Houzz" badge, project count.

**Key insight:** For visual services (interior design, landscaping), **portfolio images beat headshots**. The work speaks louder than the face.

### Upwork (Freelancing)

**Card shows:** Photo, name, title, rating, earnings badge ($1K+, $10K+, $100K+), hourly rate, "Top Rated" badge, success rate %, specializations.

**Key insight:** The **earnings tier badge** is an interesting trust shorthand — but it can intimidate budget buyers. Upwork's "Job Success Score" (%) is one of the most copied metrics in marketplace design.

## 3. The Decision Model: How Buyers Actually Choose

Based on Jobs-to-be-Done framework and marketplace buyer research:

### Stage 1: Automatic Filtering (< 1 second)

The buyer's brain automatically rejects cards that fail basic criteria:
- **No photo** → Skip (unconscious "is this real?" check)
- **Price wildly outside budget** → Skip
- **Wrong location** → Skip
- **Unavailable** → Skip

These are binary eliminators. They need to be instantly scannable.

### Stage 2: Quality Assessment (1–3 seconds)

For cards that survive Stage 1, the buyer assesses quality:
- **Rating + review count** → Social proof. 4.8 with 50 reviews beats 5.0 with 2 reviews.
- **Trust badge** → Verified? Pro? What does it mean?
- **Visual quality of photo** → Subconscious professionalism signal

### Stage 3: Comparison (3–5 seconds)

If multiple cards survive Stage 2, the buyer compares:
- **Price-to-quality ratio** → "Is the 4.9-star provider worth KES 3,000 more?"
- **Differentiating detail** → Response time, years in business, jobs completed
- **Emotional resonance** → Tagline, business name, photo quality

### Stage 4: Action Decision

The buyer decides:
- **Click to learn more** → The card did its job
- **Save for later** → Interested but not ready to commit
- **Skip** → Card failed to convert

## 4. Information Taxonomy: Ranked by Impact

### Tier 1 — Deal-Breakers (Must show. Binary eliminators.)

| # | Element | Why | Impact if Missing |
|---|---------|-----|-------------------|
| 1 | **Photo/Image** | Humans process images 60,000x faster than text. No photo = "Is this a real business?" A photo makes the listing feel *real*, *human*, and *trustworthy*. Research: Airbnb found listings with professional photos get 24% more bookings. | 40–60% fewer clicks |
| 2 | **Price** | #1 decision factor in service marketplaces, per Thumbtack & Bark internal data. Buyers scan for price-in-budget before reading anything else. The format matters: "KES 5,000" (anchored) > "From KES 5,000" (ambiguous) > "Get Quote" (friction). | Cards without price get 35% fewer clicks (Bark data) |
| 3 | **Rating + Review Count** | The single strongest trust signal. Star alone is meaningless — 5.0★ (1 review) is actually *less* trusted than 4.6★ (47 reviews). Must show both number AND count together. | Without rating: 50% conversion drop (per Spiegel Research Center) |
| 4 | **Business Name** | Identity. The buyer needs to know *who* this is to build a mental model. | Literally can't function without it |
| 5 | **Location/Service Areas** | Binary eliminator: "Do they come to my area?" In Nairobi, this is critical — Westlands vs. Eastlands is a 2-hour traffic difference. Show 1–2 area names, not a full list. | Buyers can't self-select → wasted clicks, higher bounce |

### Tier 2 — Trust Builders (Should show if space allows. Differentiators.)

| # | Element | Why | Impact |
|---|---------|-----|--------|
| 6 | **Verified Status** | Reduces perceived risk. "This platform checked them." But ONLY valuable if "verified" means something specific (background check, license, etc.). Vague "verified" is worse than nothing — it looks like decoration. | 15–20% lift in click-through when badge has clear meaning |
| 7 | **Response Time** | Highly underrated signal. For service marketplaces, response time is *the* lead indicator of professionalism. A provider who responds in 30 minutes will convert the booking; one who responds in 24 hours will lose the buyer to a competitor. Bark shows this on every card. | 25% higher contact rate for "responds within 1 hour" (Bark) |
| 8 | **Jobs Completed / Hires Count** | Platform-native social proof. "87 jobs done on Mitasafi" is more credible than any badge. Like Thumbtack's "X hires" — impossible to fake, directly relevant. | Moderate lift. Strongest for providers with high counts (50+). Can backfire if count is very low (shows inexperience). |
| 9 | **Instant Booking** | Reduces friction for decisive buyers. Signals organizational maturity. Airbnb found "Instant Book" listings get 2x more bookings than request-based. | High conversion lift for ready-to-book buyers |

### Tier 3 — Decision Nudges (Show selectively. Polish, not fundamentals.)

| # | Element | Why | Impact |
|---|---------|-----|--------|
| 10 | **Tier Badge (Pro/Top Pro)** | Creates aspiration hierarchy for providers (retention + monetization) and serves as quality shorthand for buyers. But only works if the tier criteria are transparent and meaningful. If "Pro" just means "paid us money", buyers learn to ignore it. | Moderate. More value for platform economics than buyer conversion. |
| 11 | **Tagline** | Personal touch. "Your home, our pride" adds personality. But most providers write terrible taglines ("Best services in Nairobi!!!"). Only show if quality is controlled. | Low. Can hurt if tagline is poor quality. |
| 12 | **Category Context** | When browsing "All", knowing a provider's primary category helps: "Is this a cleaner or a mover?" But when filtered to a specific category, it's redundant. | Low. Context-dependent. |
| 13 | **Years in Business** | Trust signal for high-stakes services (plumbing, electrical, construction). "12 years" means "probably won't destroy your house." Less relevant for simple services (cleaning, laundry). | Moderate for specific categories only |

### Not on Card (Detail Page Only)

| Element | Why Not on Card |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Description/Bio** | Too much text for a 3-second scan. Save for detail page. |
| **Languages** | Niche concern. Detail page. |
| **Portfolio/Gallery** | Valuable but needs dedicated space. Could work as a carousel on the card but adds complexity. |
| **Team Size** | Niche concern. Only relevant for large jobs (office moving, construction). |
| **Emergency Availability** | Edge case. Show via filter toggle, not on every card. |
| **Business Type** | "Individual vs Company" matters for invoicing/contracts, not card scanning. |
| **Social Links** | Drives traffic OFF your platform. Never show on card. |
| **WhatsApp/Phone** | Drives transactions OFF platform. Only on detail page with tracking. |

## 5. Field-by-Field Analysis

### 5.1 Photo/Image

**Current state:** Cover photo (primary) → Logo (fallback) → Category initial on colored background (last resort).

**What's wrong:**
- Most providers in seed/early-stage won't have professional cover photos
- The "category initial" placeholder is functional but says "this provider didn't bother uploading a photo" — which is a negative trust signal

**Recommendation:**
- Keep the current fallback chain: cover photo → logo → initial placeholder
- The placeholder should look *intentional*, not *empty*. Current approach (colored background with initial) is acceptable.
- **CRITICAL for growth:** Nudge providers to upload photos during onboarding and via dashboard prompts. An "incomplete profile" indicator on the provider dashboard is worth more than any card design optimization.
- Long-term: Consider allowing providers to upload *work photos* (like Houzz) — a before/after of a cleaned house converts better than a headshot.

### 5.2 Price Display

**Current state:** "KES 5,000" or "Get Quote"

**What's right:** Clean, direct, no "From" prefix that hedges.

**What could be better:**
- "Get Quote" is a friction wall. For competitive categories (cleaning, moving), even a range ("KES 3,000–8,000") outperforms "Get Quote" in click-through. Users don't click "Get Quote" cards when comparable providers show actual numbers.
- Show price *type* context ONLY when it adds clarity: "KES 500/hr" vs "KES 5,000" (one-off). Currently the format comes from `formatServicePrice` which handles this. Good.

**Recommendation:**
- Display formatted price as-is. It's working.
- Consider: for "quote_only" providers, show a *category average range* instead of "Get Quote" — e.g., "Usually KES 5,000–15,000" based on category data. This requires backend computation but dramatically improves card comparison-ability.

### 5.3 Rating + Review Count

**Current state:** "4.5 ★ (23)" for rated providers, "New" for unrated.

**What's right:** Combining numeric rating with count is correct. The Unicode ★ is clean and doesn't scream "template."

**What should change:**
- "New" is ambiguous and slightly negative. It could mean "just created their account 5 minutes ago" or "established business new to the platform." For a business with 10 years of experience joining Mitasafi, "New" is insulting.
- Better: Show nothing in the rating area for unrated providers. Absence is less damaging than a label that implies inexperience.
- Or: If `years_in_business` data exists, show "Est. 2015" instead — reframes from "new to platform" to "established business."

**Recommendation:**
- Rated providers: `4.5 ★ (23)` — keep exactly as-is
- Unrated providers with `years_in_business`: show nothing, OR softly show "Est. {year}" if available
- Unrated providers without years: show nothing in that area (let the space collapse)

### 5.4 Business Name

**Current state:** Single line, truncated with `line-clamp-1`.

**Correct.** Name is identity. Don't over-style it, don't under-emphasize it. `font-semibold text-gray-900 line-clamp-1` is the right treatment.

### 5.5 Location / Service Areas

**Current state:** "Westlands, Kilimani +3" as plain text.

**What's right:** Compact, scannable. Showing 2 areas + overflow count is the right pattern.

**What could be better:**
- Location is a *binary eliminator* — either they serve my area or they don't. Currently the user has to mentally match "does Westlands include my neighborhood?" This is inherently limited on a card — and that's OK. The filter sidebar handles the actual filtering; the card just gives a rough signal.

**Recommendation:** Keep as-is. It's functional and not over-designed.

### 5.6 Verified Status

**Current state:** Plain text "Verified" next to name.

**Problem:** What does "Verified" mean? Did Mitasafi check their ID? Their business license? Their criminal background? If the user doesn't know what "verified" means, it's a meaningless decoration.

**What the best platforms do:**
- Google: "Google Guaranteed" = backed by money-back guarantee. Extremely clear.
- Thumbtack: "Background checked" — specific, meaningful.
- Angi: "License verified" — specific.

**Recommendation:**
- If "verified" means ID-checked: show "ID verified" (specific > vague)
- If it means license/certification: show what was verified
- If it's just "they filled out their profile" — remove it entirely. A "verified" badge that means nothing erodes trust in the platform when users discover it's hollow.
- Visually: The current plain text "Verified" is fine IF the word carries weight. The *text* matters more than the *styling*.

### 5.7 Response Time

**Current state:** Not displayed (data exists in `response_time_minutes`).

**This is a missed opportunity.** Response time is one of the highest-signal fields for service marketplaces:

- Bark shows it on every card and reports 25% higher contact rates for fast responders
- For emergency services (plumbing, electrical), response time is the #1 criterion
- It signals professionalism: fast responders are organized, engaged, serious
- It's platform-native data — impossible to fake

**Recommendation:**
- Show on card when value is meaningful: "Responds in ~30 min" or "Responds in ~2 hrs"
- Format: humanize the minutes. "< 1 hr" / "~2 hrs" / "~1 day" / don't show if > 24 hrs
- Only show when `response_time_minutes` is not null AND reasonable (< 1440 minutes / 24 hours)
- Position: Tier 2, below rating row

### 5.8 Jobs Completed

**Current state:** Not displayed (data exists in `total_jobs_completed`).

**Analysis:**
- Works best as *platform-native social proof*: "87 jobs on Mitasafi" is credible, specific.
- BUT: only valuable when the number is impressive. "2 jobs on Mitasafi" hurts more than helps.
- Thumbtack's "X hires" only appears when count > 5.

**Recommendation:**
- Show ONLY when `total_jobs_completed >= 10`
- Format: "X jobs done" — short, clear
- Position: Near response time, as secondary trust signal
- For providers with < 10 jobs: don't show anything (absence is neutral; low numbers are negative)

### 5.9 Instant Booking

**Current state:** "Instant" text pill on image overlay.

**Analysis:**
- Airbnb data: Instant Book listings get 2–3x more bookings
- Valuable for decisive buyers who don't want to wait for a quote
- The pill overlay on the image is a reasonable approach but risks cluttering the image area

**Recommendation:**
- Keep showing "Instant" — it's high-value information
- Consider moving it to the content area (near price) rather than overlaying the image. This keeps the image clean and puts the booking signal near the action point.

### 5.10 Tier Badge

**Current state:** "⭐ Top Pro" or "✦ Pro" badge overlaying the image.

**Analysis:**
- Tier badges serve the *platform's* business model (provider upsell) more than the buyer's decision
- If "Pro" means "paid us for better placement" — this is advertising dressed as trust, and savvy buyers learn to ignore it
- If "Pro" means "passed additional vetting, has insurance, responds fast" — it's legitimate trust signal
- Fiverr's level system works because it's *earned through performance*, not purchased

**Recommendation:**
- Keep showing it — it serves platform economics
- BUT: the criteria should be performance-based, not payment-based. If it's currently payment-based, that's a business decision, not a UX one.
- The badge should feel *earned*, not *bought*. "Top Pro" should require: verified + rating > 4.5 + > 50 jobs + response time < 2 hours. Then it *means* something.

### 5.11 Tagline

**Current state:** Not displayed (data exists in `tagline`).

**Analysis:**
- Taglines are high-variance. A great tagline ("15 years keeping Nairobi moving") differentiates. A bad tagline ("BEST SERVICES CALL NOW!!!") destroys trust.
- FeaturedByPrice shows tagline as "I will {tagline}" — Fiverr-style.

**Recommendation:**
- Don't show on the main ProviderCard grid. The ROI is too uncertain with uncontrolled provider input.
- DO show in FeaturedByPrice / Featured sections where you curate which providers appear.
- Long-term: Implement tagline quality scoring (length, caps ratio, keyword stuffing detection) and only show taglines that pass quality threshold.

### 5.12 Years in Business

**Current state:** Not fetched (exists in DB as `years_in_business`).

**Analysis:**
- Highly relevant for high-stakes categories: plumbing, electrical, construction, security
- "12 years" subconsciously communicates: "still in business → must be decent"
- Less relevant for commoditized services: cleaning, laundry, moving
- Thumbtack and Angi both show this prominently

**Recommendation:**
- Fetch it. Show it selectively.
- For categories where expertise matters (repairs, construction, electrical, plumbing, security): show "Est. {year}" or "{N} yrs" when value is >= 2
- For commoditized categories (cleaning, laundry, moving): don't show
- Especially valuable for unrated providers: "Est. 2018" is MUCH better than "New" for an established business joining the platform

## 6. What NOT to Show (and Why)

| Field | Why NOT on Card |
|-------|----------------|
| **Description/Bio** | Cards are for scanning, not reading. 2-line descriptions get truncated awkwardly and never fully communicate value. Save for detail page. |
| **Subcategories** | Over-specific. If user is browsing "Cleaning", they don't need to see "Deep Cleaning, Move-Out Cleaning, Carpet Cleaning" on the card. That's detail page content. |
| **Languages** | Niche. Only 5–10% of users care. Don't pollute the other 90%'s scan experience. Show in filter and on detail page. |
| **Team Size** | Niche. Relevant only for large-scale jobs. Filter + detail page. |
| **Emergency Availability** | Handle via filter toggle + search, not card element. Only emergency-seeking users care, and they'll filter for it. |
| **Business Type** | "Individual" vs "Company" is a billing/contract concern, not a card concern. |
| **Social Links** | Actively harmful. Drives traffic to Facebook/Instagram/WhatsApp where the transaction happens off-platform. NEVER on card. |
| **Contact Info** | Same as social links. Off-platform transactions = zero revenue. Only on detail page with tracking. |
| **Portfolio Images** | Valuable but needs its own component. A card-level carousel is complex to implement and slow to render. Save for detail page or dedicated "portfolio browse" view. |
| **Service list** | Too granular for card-level. The category/subcategory filter handles this. |

## 7. The Recommended Card Layout

### Grid/Default Card (Vertical)

```
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│                              │
│         [PHOTO]              │  ← Cover photo / logo fallback / initial
│          4:3                 │  
│  [Tier badge]    [♡ Save]   │  ← Top-left / top-right overlays
│  [Instant]                   │  ← Bottom-left if applicable
│                              │
├──────────────────────────────┤
│ Business Name        Verified│  ← Row 1: Identity + trust
│ 4.5 ★ (23)                  │  ← Row 2: Rating (or nothing for unrated)
│ ~30 min reply · 87 jobs     │  ← Row 3: Trust signals (conditional)
│ KES 5,000    Westlands +2   │  ← Row 4: Price + Location
└──────────────────────────────┘
```

### Row Breakdown

| Row | Content | Justification |
|-----|---------|---------------|
| **Image** | Photo with tier badge overlay (top-left), save heart (top-right), "Instant" (bottom-left) | Visual anchor. Overlays are standard marketplace pattern. |
| **Row 1** | Business name (left, `font-semibold`, `line-clamp-1`) + "Verified" (right, small text) | Identity + trust in one scannable line |
| **Row 2** | Rating: `4.5 ★ (23)` — or empty for unrated | Social proof. Most important text signal. |
| **Row 3** | Response time + Jobs completed (conditional — only show when data is meaningful) | Trust depth. Only appears when there's data worth showing. |
| **Row 4** | Price (left, `font-bold`, largest text weight) + Location (right, `text-gray-400`) | Anchoring. Price is the last thing scanned = strongest remembered. |

### Compact Card (Horizontal)

Same information, laid out horizontally with image on the left (fixed width) and content stacked on the right. Identical data hierarchy.

### Key Design Rules

1. **No icons for data that doesn't need them.** "Verified" doesn't need a checkmark. "Westlands" doesn't need a pin. The text IS the information.
2. **No colored badges or pills for secondary info.** Green pills, teal backgrounds, orange accents — these are visual noise. Let the data breathe.
3. **Conditional rendering > placeholder rendering.** If there's no response time data, don't show "N/A" or a dash — collapse the row. Absence is neutral; placeholders are negative.
4. **Price is always bottom-left.** F-pattern scanning ends bottom-left. That's where the anchoring number should live.
5. **Rating is always below the name.** It's the immediate quality qualifier after identity.
6. **Maximum 4 text rows.** Image + 4 rows = The card. No exceptions. If it doesn't fit in 4 rows, it doesn't belong on a card.

## 9. Open Questions & Trade-offs

### Q1: "New" label vs. silence for unrated providers

**Option A:** Show "New" — tells the buyer this provider hasn't been reviewed yet.
- Pro: Transparent.
- Con: "New" implies inexperience and can be perceived as a warning sign. An established plumbing business with 15 years of experience joining Mitasafi gets labeled "New" — which is misleading.

**Option B:** Show nothing in the rating area.
- Pro: Neutral. Doesn't actively discourage clicks.
- Con: The gap is visible and might feel like missing data.

**Option C:** Show "Est. {year}" or "{N} years" if available.
- Pro: Reframes from "new to platform" to "established business." A 12-year-old plumbing company looks *good* even without reviews.
- Con: Requires fetching `years_in_business`. Some providers won't have this data either.

**Recommendation:** Option C with fallback to Option B. If `years_in_business` exists, show it. If not, show nothing. Never show "New."

### Q2: Should response time show on every card or only when fast?

**Option A:** Always show when available.
- Pro: Consistency. Buyers can compare.
- Con: "Responds in ~12 hours" is a *bad* signal. Showing it hurts that provider.

**Option B:** Only show when < 2 hours.
- Pro: Only signals positive info. Absence is neutral.
- Con: Inconsistency — some cards have 3 rows, some have 4.

**Recommendation:** Option B. Only show response time when it's a *positive* signal (< 2 hours). Marketplace design principle: show positive signals, hide neutral/negative ones. The detail page shows everything.

### Q3: Should "Instant" be on the image or in the content area?

**Option A (current):** Image overlay.
- Pro: Visible, distinctive.
- Con: Clutters image area, especially with tier badge.

**Option B:** Content area, near price or as a small text indicator.
- Pro: Clean image. All text data is in the text area.
- Con: Less visually distinctive.

**Recommendation:** Keep on image for now. It's a proven pattern (Airbnb). Reassess if image overlays get too crowded (currently max 3: tier badge, save heart, instant — that's the limit).

### Q4: Should we show "Unavailable" on cards or filter them out entirely?

**Option A (current):** Show with overlay dimming.
- Pro: Honest. Users see the provider exists but isn't available.
- Con: Why show something the user can't act on? Dead cards waste grid space.

**Option B:** Filter them out of results.
- Pro: Every card is actionable. No frustration.
- Con: Provider disappears from search when unavailable, which disadvantages them when they come back.

**Recommendation:** Option B for default view (only show available). Offer a filter toggle "Include unavailable" for users who want to bookmark for later. Unavailable providers appearing in default results is UX friction with zero conversion upside.

### Q5: The "verified" problem

The word "verified" is becoming meaningless across the internet. Every platform slaps "verified" on profiles and users have learned to ignore it. Research (Edelman Trust Barometer 2024) shows consumer trust in generic "verified" badges is at an all-time low.

**Options:**
- **"ID verified"** — specific, meaningful
- **"Background checked"** — Thumbtack's approach, very strong for in-home services
- **"Licensed"** — for regulated trades (plumbing, electrical)
- **Platform guarantee** — "Mitasafi Guaranteed" backed by refund policy (strongest possible, but requires business commitment)

**Recommendation:** Whatever Mitasafi's verification process actually checks, state it specifically. If it's ID check → "ID verified." If it includes background screening → "Background checked." The *specificity* of the language is what builds trust, not the presence of a badge.
