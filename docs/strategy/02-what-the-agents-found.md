# What Five Agents Found

*Five researchers attacked the plan by five different methods: an adversary, a historian,
an economist, a social researcher, and a contrarian. This is what survived and what did not.*

Date: 2026-10-19

Full reports: `agent-01-red-team.md`, `agent-02-graveyard.md`, `agent-03-economics.md`,
`agent-04-social.md`, `agent-05-contrarian.md`.

## The one finding that matters most

**Residents must never post the price.**

The price field blows up in two directions at once, and both land on the same person:

1. **It destroys the caretaker's income.** His money is the gap between what the landlord will
   accept and what he quotes a desperate seeker, plus viewing fees and favours. Fieldwork in
   Pipeline records it plainly: he "sends signals to his 'friendly tenants' when he is about to open
   the tap," and he "doesn't send the signals for free; there are 'payments' involved" — cash, a
   beer, and from some women "more personal favours." A public price is the one thing that kills
   that margin. And he does not need to work out which of four residents posted it. He controls the
   water, the gate and the parcels for all four.

2. **It turns the neighbours against the poster.** Identical units do not cost identical money —
   sitting tenants are grandfathered, some negotiated, some positions are worse, some paid the
   caretaker's mark-up. Publish the list and the woman in 2A paying 16k learns the unit above goes
   for 14k. She cannot blame the landlord: in one study of low-income Nairobi tenements, **more than
   75% of residents did not know who their landlord was.** So she blames the name on the post. And
   the landlord reads the same list and moves everyone to 16k at renewal.

Put together:

> **The list's first success in a block is the event that makes posting there impossible forever.**

The mechanism is self-extinguishing at exactly the point the design was built on.

**The fix is narrow and keeps most of the idea.** Residents confirm **existence and availability
only** — "yes, 3B is still empty." The price comes from the caretaker, the landlord, or **a seeker
who has already viewed it**. That last person has almost no social cost for publishing a price, and
is already doing it: Kenyan reporting documents renters in WhatsApp and Facebook groups where *"if a
house doesn't suit you, you post it for the next person."*

## What is dead

**1. Residents posting prices.** Above. Also: there is no legal protection at this rent level. The
Rent Restriction Act (Cap 296) still caps controlled tenancies at **KSh 2,500/month**, so every
tenant paying 4k–30k is outside it. No Kenyan provision against retaliatory eviction or a
retaliatory rent rise was found. One month's notice, no reason needed.

**2. Public "block rep" titles.** Sheng has no word for block rep and five for informer (*mtiaji,
mrazi, finyo, chomea wire, kuguna*). The role has no power anyone wants, is attached to a building
people leave in two years, and its one perk — early listings — is public proof you have not made it
yet. It is also differentially dangerous for women, where landlord and caretaker "routine checks"
demanding sexual favours are documented and women are constrained from objecting "lest they risk
being branded immoral." **Publish the building. Never publish the person beside it.**

**3. The sign-photo rule as a fraud defence.** Print an A4 sheet, tape it to the gate, photograph
it, take it down. Camera-capture, geotag and timestamp all pass honestly. Cost: under KSh 1,000,
against KSh 8,000–60,000 per victim. And "the reporter is never the contact" makes it worse — it
guarantees we display a number nobody vouched for, with nobody accountable in the chain.

**4. Mutual confirmation as a trust mechanism.** Every real Kenyan trust institution has the same
shape: *one named person stakes something they own, for one named person, in front of a group that
can reach them both.* A SACCO guarantor loses their own shares. A boda stage chairman can face
arrest for his riders. Four co-located strangers agreeing is **a quorum, not a vouch** — and people
read empty quorums fluently.

**5. The contributor head start.** Worth about 3.3% of its face value at the moment it is issued (a
resident searches one month in thirty), handed over at the worst point in the cycle, and it cannot
be stored. It is also time-shaped, therefore resellable — and only agents have the phones and the
hours to farm it. We would be manufacturing a small new agent.

**6. The three residency questions.** Floors, gate colour, borehole, caretaker's name are all
street-level knowledge. Worse, the building's own WhatsApp group was the strongest check in the
design, and **the caretaker is usually in that group, often as the admin.**

**7. "Free forever" as a going concern — unprecedented.** NoBroker raised $361m on the
anti-brokerage pitch; by 2024 the reporting headline was *"NoBroker is no longer no broker"* —
owners now pay for tenant data. QuintoAndar takes a month's rent plus ~8% monthly. Mamikos sells
owner subscriptions. **No rental listings platform in any market reached scale and still took money
from nobody.** Locally, StayNai charges tenants KSh 299/month to unlock the caretaker's number,
Kejanova escrows the viewing fee, 254rent charges KSh 1,500 per viewing. Everyone who diagnosed the
gate correctly then rebuilt the gate and stood in it.

**8. The belief that tenant cold-start was the risk.** It is not. **Every documented death in this
space was a supply death.** Spleet's founder: tenants kept coming, "it couldn't match up on the
landlords' end." Hauzisha quit Kenyan rentals calling the market "large, fast-moving." And
**KejaHunt** — Kenya, 2013, out of Nailab, explicitly for "low and middle income earners finding
affordable housing", this exact market — went free listings, then charged per human search, and is
recorded out of business in March 2021.

## What survives

**1. The building as the unit.** All five agents kept it. The social researcher strengthened it:
one accountable *mwenyeji wa GG House* beats four anonymous confirmers.

**2. Resident confirmation of availability — anonymously, with no price and no name.** This is a
real mechanism and nobody else has it. It just has to be stripped back to the one fact residents can
safely give.

**3. Freshness discipline.** SpaceYako, a live Kenyan operator, expires listings at 60 days and says
it does not verify ownership. A 7-day decay is a genuine difference, not a slogan.

**4. Never touching money.** Confirmed as a real shield — against fraud, and against the Estate
Agents Act. It offers the user nothing by itself, but it costs nothing and it keeps you out of two
kinds of trouble.

**5. The hidden-cost problem (problem 5) is the sharpest thing in the original analysis.** Published
Kasarani bedsitter rents range **KSh 9,000 to 40,000** across sources — 4.4×, no stated method. And
HassConsult's index begins at a KSh 201,832 average. **Nobody measures the KSh 4,000–30,000 band at
all.**

**6. Harvesting the seeker who just moved.** The one mechanism that got stronger under attack: the
person who has already viewed has the lowest social cost for publishing a price, is already doing it
informally, and is the only manufacturable source of ground truth.

## Four methods, one answer: the caretaker

They converged without coordinating:

- **History:** QuintoAndar's CTO says a referral programme for building **doormen** kick-started
  inventory. Survivors paid the gatekeeper (QuintoAndar), hired inspectors (Mamikos' "Mamichecker"),
  or sold to agents (BuyRentKenya, Property24). **Nobody survived on crowdsourcing.** Waze's
  contributor rate was **0.18%**, and Waze ran on passive data — a vacancy report has no passive
  equivalent.
- **Red team:** he is the cheapest enemy and the cheapest ally. One sentence in a landlord WhatsApp
  group — *"this app is how KRA finds you"* — and caretakers pull the signs. Supply has no defence.
- **Economics:** he holds the deposit refund, which is KSh 10,000–20,000 of hostage. That is why a
  resident rationally says nothing.
- **Contrarian:** one caretaker per twenty units versus twenty tenants per twenty units. A 20×
  cheaper target.

**The Kenyan doorman is the caretaker.** He is the source. Residents are the referees, not the
source. And if he is not a declared party with something to gain, he is the assassin.

Important caveat: **no evidence was found that Kenyan caretakers already take vacancy finder's
fees.** There is plenty on caretaker rent theft and forged leases, nothing on finder's fees. So the
doorman tactic is a hypothesis for Kenya, not a finding. **Two hours of conversation settles it.**

## Two things the economics says, both uncomfortable

**Everything this can give a tenant is worth about KSh 292 a month.** On a KSh 10,000 unit, search
and entry cost roughly KSh 8,750 per move, spread over a 30-month tenancy. **A 4% rent rise cancels
all of it.** And only the KSh 1,500 of fares is value genuinely destroyed with nobody claiming it —
the rest is a transfer, and transfers relocate, because bargaining power is set by the
seeker-to-vacancy ratio, which we do not change.

**And transparency may be the source of that 4%.** In October 1993 the Danish Competition Council
began publishing firm-level transaction prices for ready-mixed concrete, to help buyers. Prices of
the reported grades **rose 15–20%** in a year while other prices moved 1–2%; the authors tested and
rejected the obvious alternative explanations. A transparency measure meant to help buyers handed
sellers a coordination device. **Our relevant market is not Nairobi — it is the six caretakers
around one matatu stage.** That is cartel-sized.

Counter-evidence, stated fairly: Craigslist is credited with cutting rental vacancy about 11%, and
one study found larger brokers associated with *lower* rents.

**The design rule: the seeker sees many prices; the landlord sees one.** Publish all-in cost as
coarse quarterly distributions by estate and unit type. **Never ship a live, sortable, per-building
rent table.** That resolves the direct conflict between two of the agents — the price index is the
right asset, at the wrong granularity if you build it the obvious way.

**And one observation that reframes the whole villain:** cutting a void from 6 weeks to 2 on a KSh
10,000 unit recovers **KSh 9,238** — approximately one month's rent. **The agent's commission is
priced at the value of the void he prevents.** He is not an arbitrary parasite. Removing him
liberates only the gap between his fee and his actual effort.

## How to frame it, and the words to use

**Nyumba kumi is the warning.** In **Kayole, 86% knew about it** and it still failed on trust and
participation. Most citizens cannot tell it apart from community policing; scholarship records it as
surveillance. **Awareness is not adoption — a big Facebook launch buys the 86%, not the
participation.** Where community policing is credited, the frame is always protection from an
outside threat.

So: **frame it as protecting each other from fake agents and viewing fees. Never as reporting what
is in your building.**

- **Avoid:** *ripoti* / "report" (police register), *thibitisha* (officialese), *balozi* (the nyumba
  kumi title), *mlinzi* for caretaker (that is the guard), and *bure* on its own (it also means
  worthless, and reads as bait — Kenyan fact-checks find the scam fingerprint is an institutional
  tone plus "free").
- **Use:** "vacant" (the real market word), *weka hapa*, *nimeona / iko bado*, "caretaker",
  *mwenyeji wa GG House* or no title at all, *mtu wa maana*, and the anchor: **"hakuna viewing
  fee."**

Confirm Sheng with native speakers per estate — the dictionaries themselves warn the vocabulary
turns over fast.

**And a better reason for this product to exist than cutting out agents:** the factual fields.
Floor. Lift or no lift. Ground-floor availability. Gate hours. Which days the water runs. That
spares a wheelchair user, a single mother and a gay tenant the fare *and* the refusal at the
doorstep. Transparency does not remove discrimination — a documented Kenyan case has a tenant
refused in Pangani **at signing**, over a Luo surname. Removing the search gatekeeper moves the
filter to the phone call and the gate, which makes it cheaper to apply and more expensive to suffer.
The factual fields are the part that genuinely helps.

## The test that matters, and the one that lies

**The lying test:** "can three residents of one building confirm each other off one Facebook post in
14 days?" **LeaseSwap NYC** — tenant-reported vacancies, free, reciprocity-gated, seeded on social
media, the closest analogue anywhere — got **~100 entries in a single day** from one Reddit post.
Then it stalled at **300 listings and 1,300 users**, in the most desperate rental market in America,
with free national press. The founder's moderation time went on **deactivating brokers trying to get
in.** By October 2026 it had become a scraper. **The cold start was never the risk. The ceiling
was.**

**The honest test, at month 3:** phone the contact on 50 listings. **If more than half are agents or
brokers, you have rebuilt BuyRentKenya with no revenue.**

## Three measurements this week. No code, no money.

**1. Count the groups.** Nobody could find a single member count, founding date, admin identity or
fee practice for any Kenyan Facebook or Telegram house-hunting group — the only hard figure anywhere
was one WhatsApp group with **54 members**. Your whole distribution plan rests on this. Join ten
groups, count members, note who admins them, whether they charge for posts, and what happens to
someone who posts a fake. **One afternoon.**

**2. Ask five caretakers one question.** *"If somewhere sent you tenants who already knew the price
and were ready to view, would you tell it when a unit goes empty?"* Then: what do you earn today,
and from whom? This tests the thing four agents converged on, and it is currently a hypothesis for
Kenya rather than a fact. **Two hours.**

**3. Run the rent-truth survey.** Not advertised price — **all-in**: rent, water, garbage, service
charge, deposit, goodwill. One post, images rather than links, in the biggest groups. This is the
only unoccupied asset anyone found, and it needs no field work at all.

## The fork

Here is the thing to face. **Every death in this space was a supply death, supply means the
caretaker, and the caretaker means field work — which you have said you do not want to do.**

That leaves two honest shapes:

**A. Accept a small amount of field work.** Not walking estates and photographing signs — that part
stays dead. Just a standing relationship with five to ten caretakers in **one** estate. That is the
QuintoAndar doorman playbook, it is the only move with a documented record of solving cold-start
supply, and it is days of work rather than months.

**B. Build the voice instead of the listings.** Own the price truth and the scam warnings — the
Kenyan rent report, the all-in cost index, the number you check before you call. It needs no field
work, it fits the channel you actually have, and the precedent is real: one LinkedIn housing group
reached **~6,000 members with no product at all**, on nothing but a broker-fee ban and real names.
**The group outgrew the app.** It is also the only honest route into deposit lending later, because
nobody underwrites a KSh 30,000 moving loan without knowing the unit, the rent and the landlord are
real.

**My recommendation: do measurement 2 before choosing.** Five caretaker conversations, two hours,
and it decides the fork. If caretakers will feed a free list that brings them ready tenants, take A
and the whole design works with the caretaker at the centre. If they will not, A has no supply and
B is the only honest answer.

Do not start building either until that conversation has happened.
