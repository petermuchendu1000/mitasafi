# Mitasafi — How the Community Builds and Runs It

*This replaces the "walk the estate yourself" plan in `00-first-principles.md`, Part 5.
Written after the founder said: I don't want to do it myself. I can post in big Facebook and
WhatsApp groups. The community should build and run this.*

Date: 2026-10-19

---

## Part 0 — What changed, and why it is better

My first plan said: walk one estate, photograph every sign, build the list by hand.

That plan had a weak spot. It made **you** the person who checks everything. That does not grow,
and it dies the week you get tired.

Your idea fixes it. Here it is in one sentence:

> **The people who live in a building are the ones who check that building.**

Someone lives in GG House. A unit there is empty. They walk past it every morning. Checking costs
them ten seconds. And look at what they already know, for free:

- Whether it is actually empty, or someone moved in on Saturday
- The real price, because they pay it themselves
- The caretaker's real name and real number
- What the water is like, what the gate is like, whether there are power problems

No agent can match that. No paid walker can match that. **And they have good reasons to help:**
an empty unit next door is bad for them — worse security, service charge split fewer ways — and a
neighbour they helped choose is better than a stranger. Plus everybody gets asked *"unajua nyumba?"*
by a cousin or a workmate. This gives them a good answer.

So the design centre moves. It is no longer "listings." It is **buildings, with their own residents
watching them.**

**One honest limit, up front.** The community can own the data, the checking and most of the
policing. It cannot own, on day one, the software, the rules, or the first two weeks of someone
being present. Part 6 is about that, with no sugar-coating.

## Part 1 — The building is the thing

Not the listing. **The building.** GG House. Plot 14. Mama Njeri's plot. Githurai Court.

A building is anything with one gate and one caretaker. It could be a 40 unit block or a plot with
six single rooms. People already name these places and already know the names.

Why the building is the right unit:

| Listings | Buildings |
|---|---|
| Gone in a week | Stay for 30 years |
| Nobody owns them | Have residents who live there |
| Must be checked one by one | Check once, covers 20+ units forever |
| No natural community | **Already have a WhatsApp group** |

That last row is the whole thing. Most Kenyan apartment blocks already have a residents' WhatsApp
group. **That group already exists, already has the right people in it, and already talks about who
is moving out.** You are not building a community. You are plugging into thousands of small
communities that are already running.

## Part 2 — The loop that makes it grow without you

This is the most important part of this document.

```
   Someone claims their building  ──►  Their neighbour claims it too
                │                                  │
                │                      they now confirm each other
                ▼                                  ▼
   "Any empty unit in your block?"  ──►  "Yes, unit 3B, 14k"
                │                                  │
                ▼                                  ▼
       Seeker gets an alert  ──►  Goes to see it  ──►  Moves in
                                                       │
                                                       ▼
                                   NOW THEY LIVE IN A BUILDING
                                                       │
                                                       ▼
                                  They claim it. They become a checker.
                                  In a building nobody was watching before.
```

Read the bottom of that loop again. **Every person who finds a house becomes a new checker, in a
new building.** The thing the system produces is the thing the system feeds on.

That is why this can grow without you. You are not adding checkers. Housing people adds checkers.
One person housed in a new block opens a block nobody was watching. One person housed in a block you
already cover makes that block stronger.

No listings site in Kenya has this. They house someone and the person leaves forever. You house
someone and they stay, as a watchman for their own gate.

## Part 3 — The first ask is not "post a house"

This is where most community projects die. They open with the hard ask.

**Wrong first ask:** "Post a vacant house." That needs the person to know of one, right now. Most
people don't. So they scroll past, and you look empty, and nobody comes back.

**Right first ask:** **"Which building do you live in? Claim it."**

Look at how much better this is:

- Takes 10 seconds. From the sofa. Nobody walks anywhere.
- Everybody can do it. You don't need to know of an empty house. You just need to live somewhere.
- It feels good. You become the person who represents GG House.
- Kenyans **love** naming their estate and their block. Ask the question in a Facebook group and
  you will get hundreds of comments.
- And it quietly builds the checking network **before** you need it.

By the time you ask "anything empty in your block?", there are already four people in GG House who
can answer and confirm each other. You built the referees before the match started.

**The order matters. Claim first. Report second.** Get this backwards and nothing works.

## Part 4 — The trust ladder

Every listing shows exactly where it stands. Four levels. All free.

| Level | What it means | What the seeker sees |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One person posted it. We don't know them | **Not confirmed** — be careful |
| 2 | Someone who says they live in that building posted it | **Reported by a resident** |
| 3 | A second resident of the same building agreed | **Confirmed by neighbours ✓** |
| 4 | Someone went there and saw it | **Seen today ✓✓** |

Plus the rule from the first document: **everything drops back down after 7 days unless someone
confirms it again.**

Why this works better than any "verified" badge:

- It is **true**. You are not claiming to have checked anything you didn't check.
- It tells the seeker exactly how much risk they are taking. They decide.
- It gives the community a job with a visible result. Confirming a listing moves it up a level, in
  public, and everyone can see you did it.
- **No competitor can copy it.** The portals would have to admit most of their listings are level 1.

Say what each level means in plain words inside the product. Never say "verified." Say *"two people
who live in this block say it was empty this morning."*

## Part 5 — How do you know someone really lives in GG House?

This is the real attack. If anyone can claim to live in a building, a fraudster claims it and
confirms his own fake listing.

Five defences, all free, all without documents:

**1. People confirm each other.**
The first person to claim a building is not verified. When a second person claims the same building
and their answers match, they verify each other. A third makes it solid. A lone claim on a building
nobody else has claimed stays weak — and so does anything they post. **A fraudster would have to
bring three fake accounts to one real building, and then say the same true things about it.**

**2. Questions only a resident can answer.**
When you claim a building, answer three small things: how many floors? What colour is the gate? Is
there a borehole or is it tanker water? What does the caretaker's name start with? Then compare the
answers of everyone claiming that building. A person outside the gate gets these wrong. **It costs
nothing to ask and it filters out almost everyone who doesn't live there.**

**3. The invite comes from inside.**
A resident of GG House shares the link in **the GG House WhatsApp group.** Anybody who joins through
that link starts with trust, because a real resident put it there. This is also how it spreads — one
resident, one group, twenty neighbours.

**4. Time.**
Someone who has been right about GG House for three months lives in GG House. Fraudsters do not
wait three months. So let trust build slowly and give new accounts almost none.

**5. Photos from inside the compound.**
Taken in the app, with location, right then. No uploading old pictures. A person who cannot get past
the gate cannot take that photo.

**What this gives you for free:** you also end up with verified caretakers. *"The caretaker of GG
House is John, 07xx — confirmed by 4 residents."* That is identity checking with no documents, no
licence, no office. The residents did it.

## Part 6 — What the community can own, and what it cannot

You said: built and managed by the community, not me. Mostly yes. But let's be exact, because
pretending otherwise would waste your time.

| The job | Who | Honest answer |
|---|---|---|
| Finding empty houses | **Community** | Yes, completely |
| Confirming they are empty | **Community** | Yes, completely — this is the GG House idea |
| Confirming the price | **Community** | Yes — residents know, and movers correct it |
| Confirming who the caretaker is | **Community** | Yes |
| Removing junk and fake posts | **Community** | Yes, with the ladder in Part 7 |
| Settling arguments | **Community** | Yes, inside their own estate |
| Welcoming new people | **Community** | Yes, and they are better at it than you |
| Posting in Facebook and WhatsApp groups | **You, then them** | You start it. Within weeks, members post for you — that's how it spreads |
| **Writing the rules** | **You** | A community cannot invent its own rules from nothing. Somebody must write them first |
| **Building and fixing the software** | **You / a developer** | The community cannot own a server. Someone must answer when it breaks |
| **Handling scam cases and police matters** | **You + a small council** | Too serious to spread thin |

So the truthful sentence is not "the community builds it." It is:

> **You build the machine and write the rules. The community does all the work inside it. Then you
> shrink your job on purpose.**

Every successful thing like this worked that way. Wikipedia had one person write the early rules
before millions wrote the articles. Someone had to go first. Going first is not the same as doing
it all forever.

## Part 7 — Giving away power, step by step

The trick that makes community management actually work: **nobody ever gets power over the whole
country. Only over their own gate, or their own estate.**

That means no job is big, and nobody can do big damage.

| Level | Who | What they can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Anyone** | A new user | Post, confirm, flag, search |
| **Resident** | 2+ neighbours agree you live there | Your confirmation counts more. You can mark things taken **in your building only** |
| **Block rep** | The longest-standing accurate resident | Edit your building's details. Approve new residents of your block |
| **Area rep** | A few blocks, or one estate | Settle arguments, remove junk, recruit block reps **in that area only** |
| **Council** | You + 5 to 10 area reps | Write the rules. Handle scam cases. Nothing else |

Rules that keep it clean:

- **Two block reps per building, never one.** One person alone becomes a small tyrant.
- **Fixed terms.** Six months, then it rotates.
- **Every decision is visible.** Who removed what, and why. Written down.
- **You can appeal upwards.** Block → area → council.
- **Power is earned by being right**, measured by things you confirmed that turned out true.
- **Nobody is paid.** The moment someone is paid, people start posting rubbish to earn.

## Part 8 — Your Facebook and WhatsApp plan

This is your real asset, so let's use it properly. The big mistake would be posting "download our
app." Nobody will. And Facebook buries posts with links.

**What spreads on Facebook is comments.** So ask questions whose answers are the data you need.
Every comment is both engagement and a database row.

### Post 1 — Claim your building

> *Which apartment or plot do you live in? Just comment the name and the estate. For example:
> "GG House, Pipeline".*
>
> *We are building a free list of empty houses — checked by the people who actually live in those
> buildings. No agents. No viewing fees. Nobody asks you for a shilling.*
>
> *If you live somewhere, you already know things nobody else knows. That's all we need.*

Expect hundreds of comments. Every one is a building. **You need zero software to run this post.**
The comments are your first database.

### Post 2 — A few days later, same group

> *Thank you — [number] buildings so far.*
>
> *Now: is there an empty unit in your block right now? Comment the building name, the type
> (bedsitter / 1 bedroom / 2 bedroom) and the price.*
>
> *Your neighbours will confirm it. That is the whole point — nobody can lie about a block where
> other people live.*

### Post 3 — The one that spreads

> *14 houses were confirmed empty today, by people who live in those buildings.*
>
> *Pipeline — bedsitter — 7,500 — confirmed by 3 neighbours*
> *Githurai 44 — 1 bedroom — 11,000 — confirmed by 2 neighbours*
> *Kayole — 2 bedroom — 15,000 — seen today*
> *(…)*
>
> *Free. No agent. No viewing fee. Nobody on this list should ever ask you for money before you are
> standing inside the house. If they do, tell us.*

That post is the first time anybody in Kenya has read that sentence. **That is what gets shared.**

### Post 4 — Ask for help, by name

> *We need two people per building to keep this honest. If you live in a block and you are willing
> to answer "is it still empty?" when a neighbour asks — say so below. You'll be the Mitasafi rep
> for your block.*

This is where your block reps come from. **The people who commented most eagerly on post 1 are your
first moderators.** Go through those comments and ask them personally.

### For WhatsApp

Different from Facebook. WhatsApp is for the building itself.

Give every resident one line they can paste into **their own building's group**:

> *Hey — there's a free list of empty houses, checked by residents. I've added our block. If
> someone asks whether a unit here is still free, just answer. No fees, no agents. [link]*

One resident, one group, twenty neighbours. **That is the cheapest growth there is, and you are not
in the room when it happens.**

### Rules for your posting

- Post at the same time every day. People start waiting for it.
- **Never ask for money. Never let anyone think you might.** Repeat it in every single post.
- Always say how many families got housed. That is the number that makes people join.
- Reply to comments for the first two weeks. After that, your block reps reply.
- When someone finds a house, say so in public and name who helped. **Thanking people in public is
  the fuel.** It costs nothing and nothing else works as well.

## Part 9 — What fraudsters will try now, and what stops them

The building model closes most of the old doors. Here is what is left.

| What they try | What stops it |
|---|---|
| Claim a building they don't live in | Real residents claim it too and contradict them. Resident questions (Part 5). Nothing they post rises above "Not confirmed" |
| Make 3 fake accounts in one building | They must get the building's real details right, from different numbers, over weeks. Possible but expensive — and real residents will notice a claim they don't recognise |
| Post a real empty house but put their own number | **The number must be the one written on the sign, in the photo.** A different number gets flagged automatically |
| Ask for a fee before viewing | Every post, every alert, every listing says we never charge. Seekers report it in one tap. The number goes on the warning list |
| Say a rival's real house is "taken" | Flagging costs you standing. Residents of that building can overrule an outsider. The person who posted it gets to confirm again first |
| Pretend to be Mitasafi | One permanent rule: *we never ask for money, for anything, ever.* Block reps are listed publicly and checkable |
| Keep an old cheap listing alive as bait | 7 day expiry. It falls off by itself |

**The strongest defence is still the simplest: you never touch money.** No deposits, no escrow, no
fees. A system that never holds money cannot be used to steal money. Say that in every post, forever.

## Part 10 — The honest cost of this design

In the first document I wrote: *never build a record that links a person to a building.*

**This design does exactly that.** If Mercy claims GG House, your database says Mercy lives in GG
House. I should say so plainly rather than quietly change my position.

It is a real cost, and it is worth paying, because resident checking is the only thing that solves
verification at zero cost. But it has to be contained:

- Store a **handle and a first name**, never a full name. Never a unit number. The building is
  enough.
- **Never show a resident to a seeker.** The seeker sees *"confirmed by 3 residents"*, never who.
- **A resident can go invisible any time**, and their past confirmations stay but stop being
  theirs.
- **Keep searching completely separate from claiming.** Searching needs no account at all. You must
  never be able to join "Mercy lives in GG House" with "Mercy is looking to move to Donholm on a
  12k budget." Those two things must live apart, by design, not by promise.
- Phone numbers are for alerts only, shown to nobody, ever.

Also: this is now clearly personal data under the Data Protection Act 2019. Write a plain notice in
English and Kiswahili, say exactly what you store and why, and let people delete themselves in one
tap. Doing this properly is free, and it is a trust signal in a market full of people who don't.

## Part 11 — What you still have to do yourself

You asked not to do it yourself. Here is the honest list of what stays yours. It is short, and none
of it is walking around estates.

**Before anything (a few days):**
1. Write the rules. One page. Plain words. What this is, what it never does, who decides what.
2. Decide the promises you will never break: free forever, never touch money, listings expire,
   never say "verified" when you mean "confirmed".

**First two weeks (about an hour a day):**
3. Post in the groups. Posts 1 to 4 above.
4. Answer comments. This is the part that cannot be skipped — early on, people need to see a human.
5. Spot your first block reps in the comments and ask them personally.

**Weeks 3 to 8 (shrinking fast):**
6. Hand replying to block reps.
7. Hand posting to area reps, who know their estates better than you anyway.
8. Keep the software working. This stays yours.

**After that:**
9. You are the council: rules, scam cases, and the server. Nothing else.

**What you never do:** walk estates, photograph signs, phone landlords, check houses, chase
listings, or moderate individual posts. All of that is theirs from day one.

That is roughly 15 to 20 hours in the first two weeks, then a few hours a week. If that is still too
much, the project cannot start — because somebody has to go first.

## Part 12 — The first 30 days, community version

**Days 1–3 — Write the rules and build nothing.**
One page of rules. The four promises. The three resident questions. Nothing else.

**Days 4–7 — Post 1, in two or three big groups.**
Collect building claims in the comments. Count them. **This answers the only question that matters
right now: will people claim their building?** If 300 people comment, you have a project. If 11 do,
the ask is wrong and you fix the words before writing any code.

**Days 8–14 — Post 2, and run it on paper.**
Empty houses come in as comments and WhatsApp messages. Put them in a spreadsheet by hand — this is
the last hand work you do. Ask the neighbours of each building to confirm. Watch whether they
actually do. **That is the second question that matters: will a neighbour confirm when asked?**

**Days 15–21 — Post 3, the first list. Then ask for reps.**
Publish the list free and dated. Watch what gets shared. Then post 4, and recruit 10 block reps from
the most active commenters.

**Days 22–30 — Decide, then build small.**
Five numbers:
- How many buildings claimed?
- How many have 2+ residents? *(this is your checking network)*
- How many empty houses reported per week?
- When neighbours were asked to confirm, what share answered?
- How many people actually moved into a house?

If those look alive, now build the smallest thing: claim a building, post an empty unit, confirm a
neighbour's post, get alerts. A web page under 300KB, no app store. Nothing else. Not maps, not
filters, not chat, not profiles.

If they look dead, **you learned it in four weeks with no code and no money.**

## Part 13 — Questions only you can answer

1. **Which Facebook and WhatsApp groups?** Name them. How many members? Will the admins let you
   post, or do you need to ask first? *(Get this wrong and nothing else matters.)*
2. **What are the three resident questions?** They must be things every resident knows and no
   outsider does. Get these right and you barely need anything else.
3. **How long will you reply to comments yourself before handing over?** Be honest with yourself.
4. **Who writes the code?** This is the one job the community cannot take. If it is not you, who?
5. **What do you do if a block rep goes bad?** Decide now, while it is theoretical.
6. **Are you willing to let the list look empty** in the early weeks, rather than filling it with
   unconfirmed posts? This is the decision that makes or breaks the whole thing.
