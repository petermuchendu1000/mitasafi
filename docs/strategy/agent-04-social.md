# Agent 04 — Social Research: Will Kenyans Confirm Vacancies in Their Own Building?

Date: 2026-10-19
Method: cultural and social assessment. Kenyan sources prioritised. Documented evidence and my own
reasoning are labelled separately throughout.

---

## The one dynamic most likely to break this

**It is not the vacancy. It is the price field.**

The proposal asks a resident to post two things: *unit 3B is empty* and *14k*. The first is gossip —
cheap, deniable, already circulating in the block. The second is a detonation, and it goes off in two
directions at once, both landing on the only person in the chain who is publicly named.

**One: it takes money out of the caretaker's pocket.** The caretaker's income is not his salary. It
is the gap between what the landlord will accept and what he tells a desperate seeker, plus the
viewing fee, the commitment fee, and the favours. Kenyan reporting documents the fee layer — letting
agents introducing "commitment fees" to prove seriousness
([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/life-and-style/dn2/money-hungry-landlords-fleece-tenants-of-their-deposit-refunds-886648)),
viewing fees of about Sh2,500 before you see anything
([Mwakilishi](https://mwakilishi.com/article/real-estate-news/2025-03-05/whatsapp-and-facebook-become-lifelines-for-house-hunters-in)),
forum complaints of fees reaching 20% of annual rent
([Kenya Talk](https://kenyatalk.com/t/viewing-fees-and-agency-fees-are-a-scam/209148)).
Slum Dwellers International's Pipeline account documents the caretaker's discretionary economy
directly: he "sends signals to his 'friendly tenants' when he is about to open the tap," he "doesn't
send the signals for free; there are 'payments' involved" — cash, a beer at the ground-floor bar,
and from some women "more personal favours" — and concludes "that's how powerful this position can
be" ([SDI](https://sdinet.org/2013/05/the-tenement-city-the-inconvenient-urban-reality-facing-nairobi/)).
A public, resident-sourced price is the one thing that destroys that margin, because it removes his
monopoly on the number. *Reasoning:* he will read the post correctly and immediately, and he does not
need to guess which of four residents wrote it — he controls the water, the gate and the parcel for
all four.

**Two: it costs the resident their neighbours.** In a Kenyan block, identical units do not cost
identical money. Long-sitting tenants are grandfathered below market; some negotiated; some took a
worse position by the bin or the gate; some were quoted high and never found out. A published price
list tells the woman in 2A, paying 16k, that the unit above her goes for 14k. She will not blame the
landlord, who is invisible — in one
qualitative study of low-income Nairobi tenements, **more than 75% of residents did not know the
identity of their landlord**, with rent collection handled by agents "usually residents within the
same housing block"
([Manchester](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/nairobi-the-socio-political-implications-of-informal-tenement-hou/)).
She will blame whoever's name is on the post.

The landlord reads the same list. *Reasoning:* once 14k and 16k are both public, the rational
landlord moves everyone to 16k at renewal. **The list's first success in a block is therefore the
event that makes posting in that block impossible forever — the mechanism is self-extinguishing at
exactly the unit the design is built on.**

Money privacy is not an assumption I am importing. Kenyan reporting on income disclosure finds young
professionals withholding figures because "discussing exact salaries with friends can stir comparison
and tension," disclosing only to "people they trusted," and fearing the obligation that follows
([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/life-and-style/mynetwork/the-big-secret-why-i-don-t-reveal-my-true-income-4631572)).
Rent is the largest line in a mass-market budget. Publishing it is publishing your status.

**The fix is cheap: the resident confirms existence and availability; the price comes from the
caretaker, the landlord, or a seeker who has already viewed — never from a named neighbour.**
Separate the fields and the system survives. Keep them joined and the best blocks burn out first.

## 1. The social cost of confirming on your own building

**The caretaker is the adversary, not the landlord.** This is the central correction. The landlord is
absent — in Pipeline, academic work finds most landlords belong to "Kenya's political-economic elite,
including members of the political class and high-ranking civil servants"
([Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13604813.2025.2471160)). He does not
know your face. The caretaker does, and he handles "building maintenance, rent collection, rent
dispute resolution (on behalf of the landlord), basic maintenance of hygiene and sometimes evictions"
([SDI](https://sdinet.org/2013/05/the-tenement-city-the-inconvenient-urban-reality-facing-nairobi/)).
Water is rationed — "intermittent and only supplied at specific days of the week" — and late rent
"can lead to water or electricity cuts"
([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/high-rise-living-in-nairobis-pipeline-estate-is-stressful-how-men-and-women-cope-265499)).

*Reasoning:* a man who decides when your tap opens, whether the gate opens at 11pm, and what he tells
the landlord at deposit inspection needs no formal power to punish a block rep. He needs only to be
slightly slower, in twenty small ways, for six months. There is no incident to report and nothing to
appeal.

**Legal protection is effectively absent at this rent level.** The Rent Restriction Act (Cap 296)
still covers only dwelling-houses whose standard rent does not exceed **KSh 2,500 per month** — the
threshold is in the current revised text
([Kenya Law](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1959/35/eng@2022-12-31)) and reviewers call it
"overtaken by events"
([CAHF](https://cahf.gitbook.io/kenya-legal-policy-and-institutional-review/annex-f-laws-governing-financing-rental-taxation/27.-rent-restriction-act-1959-cap-296)).
Every tenant in the 4k–30k band sits **outside** the controlled-tenancy regime. I found no Kenyan
provision against retaliatory eviction or retaliatory rent increase
([tenant-rights guides surveyed](https://lawguide.co.ke/landlord-and-tenant-rights-in-kenya/)). A
one-month notice is lawful and needs no reason. The honest statement to a prospective block rep is:
*if your landlord decides to move you, nothing stops him.*

**Does posting produce real trouble? Documented: yes.** The closest case is an estate WhatsApp group.
The chairperson of Samburu Court Residents' Association posted during a thefts discussion; a
neighbour complained she had implied they were "habouring thieves"; police moved to charge her
criminally with defamation alongside a civil suit. The Kibera High Court quashed it, Justice Diana
Kavedza holding that "the police should not be involved in the settlement of what is purely a civil
dispute" and that "criminal justice powers are not toys to be played around with"
([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/judge-faults-dpp-police-over-estate-whatsapp-group-row-5001898);
[Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/news/judge-furious-with-police-over-feuds-from-estate-whatsapp-groups-5018892)).
The lesson is not the verdict: **in a Kenyan estate, a post in a group can become a police visit.**
She won — after lawyers, months and a High Court petition. A Githurai tenant will not do that. The
deterrent is the OB number.

**Neighbours.** *Reasoning.* The strategy doc argues neighbours gain from a filled unit — security,
service charge split wider. In this band service charge is usually bundled into rent, so what
neighbours actually get is a stranger they did not choose, chosen by the block rep. The first
list-sourced tenant who is loud or trouble at the gate is *the block rep's* tenant: liability with no
upside. Against this, the vacancy was already being discussed in the block. The resident is not
revealing it — they are **writing it down with their name next to it.** That is the entire difference,
and the whole risk.

## 2. How vouching really works here

Every real Kenyan trust institution has the same three-part shape: **one named person stakes
something they own, for one named person, before a group that can reach them both.** Confirmation of
a *fact* by *strangers who happen to be co-located* has none of those parts.

| Institution | How a stranger gets in | What binds the vouch | Cost of vouching wrongly |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chama / merry-go-round** | Interview plus a guarantee by an existing member; chamas have "a reputation for exclusivity" ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chama_(investment))) | The voucher's standing and turn in the rotation | Shame, loss of turn, pressure to cover; group risk registers list "death of the guarantor" ([UoN](http://erepository.uonbi.ac.ke/bitstream/handle/11295/21388/Adede%20-%20Merry%20Go%20Round%20Concept%20And%20Informal%20Financial%20Markets%20In%20Kenya.pdf?sequence=3)) |
| **SACCO** | Formal guarantors, typically 2–3 | Legally enforceable; guarantor's **own shares and deposits** are attached on default ([Sacco Review](https://saccoreview.co.ke/loan-defaults-hit-guarantors-hard/); [Kenya Law 57/2021](http://kenyalaw.org/caselaw/cases/view/219202/)) | Real money. The hardest vouch in Kenyan life |
| **Boda boda stage** | Chairman, stage registration, good-conduct certificate, SACCO membership ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2025-09-12-riders-must-register-wear-uniforms-under-new-rules)) | The chairman is personally answerable and "could face arrest" if his riders offend | Crackdowns until he names those responsible. Liability is the price of the title |
| **Church / mosque** | Introduced by a member; standing accrues over months of attendance | Ongoing presence before witnesses | Reputational, slow, total within that congregation |
| **Kin — "unajua nyumba?"** | A relative asks; they inherit your judgement | The relationship itself | You own the outcome. Migration research notes "the benefits of the network come with additional social obligations" ([4Mi Nairobi](https://mixedmigration.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/238_Nairobi_City_Report_and_Annex.pdf)) |
| **Mama mboga / duka** | No vouch needed — credit rests on daily visibility | Repeated small face-to-face transactions | You lose credit, quietly |

Two conclusions.

**Kenyan trust is person-to-person and consequential, not person-to-public and reputational.** There
is no indigenous institution in which you certify a condition to an audience of strangers for a
badge. The nearest is the boda chairman — whose status comes bundled with *arrest liability*. In
Kenya a local title without liability reads as empty; with liability, as a trap.

**"Residents confirm each other" is epistemically inverted.** A vouch transfers trust from someone
with standing to someone without. This mechanism asks four people with no standing with each other —
tenants in an anonymous block, most of whom do not know their own landlord — to manufacture
credibility by agreeing. *Reasoning:* that is a quorum, not a vouch, and Kenyans read empty quorums
fluently. What maps onto local practice is the opposite shape: **one named person, reachable by
phone, who answers and says "nilikuambia" — I told you.** One accountable *mwenyeji* per building
beats four anonymous confirmers, and is cheaper to build.

## 3. What makes an unpaid local role stick

**It sticks when the role carries real discretion.** WhatsApp admin is wanted because the admin can
remove you — documented at the extreme in a Luo professionals' welfare group, where members
questioning fund management were removed from the platform, escalating into litigation
([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/news/-removed-by-admin-the-whatsapp-alert-that-exposed-luo-welfare-group-5522530)).
Estate officials control gate policy, service charge and contractors — and they hold on: Runda
residents complained committee members had served over ten years, some selected unilaterally
([Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/66032-runda-residents-raise-concerns-over-services-offered-estate)).
Chama treasurers hold money. Church committees allocate.

**It goes bad by capture.** Kenya's own feature on resident associations names the cause — "a notable
gap in their legal powers and framework," leaving room for conflict
([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/life-and-style/dn2/resident-associations-the-good-bad-and-ugly--5104990));
Parklands developers allege their residents' association used intimidation and court orders to stall
projects ([Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/city-affairs/140186/nairobi-property-developers-call-for-unity-decry-intimidation-in-parklands-during-agm)).

Now **"block rep for GG House."** *Reasoning:* it carries no discretion anyone wants — you cannot
remove, allocate or decide anything. It is scoped to a building most people intend to leave within two
years. Its only concrete privilege, earlier access to listings, is the privilege of someone still
looking for a house: visible evidence you have not made it. In Kayole or Githurai it reads as a joke,
or worse, as nyumba kumi with a logo. The vocabulary is already waiting: Kenyan
Sheng dictionaries list **mtiaji**, **razi / mrazi**, **finyo**, *chomea wire*, *kuguna* — all
meaning snitch or betrayer ([Go Sheng](https://www.sheng.co.ke/kamusi/?s_word=);
[Lughayangu](https://lughayangu.com/post/top-50-trending-and-popular-sheng-words-improve-your-vocabulary)).
There is no Sheng word for "block rep." There are five for informer.

The one role that could stick is not inside the building — it is **estate-level**: the person everyone
in Pipeline rings when they need a house. That is a recognisable Kenyan position, attractive because
it generates obligations owed *to* them. It also naturally wants to be paid, which is the honest
tension in "nobody is paid."

## 4. The nyumba kumi lesson

**Awareness is high; participation and trust are not.** A study of the initiative in **Kayole,
Nairobi** — a target estate — found **86% of respondents knew about Nyumba Kumi**, yet many distrusted
it and did not take part; cited reasons included elders being unclear on their roles and residents
doubting their leaders
([Effectiveness of the Nyumba Kumi community policing initiative in Kenya](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332528188_Effectiveness_of_the_Nyumba_Kumi_Community_Policing_Initiative_In_Kenya)).
A National Crime Research Centre finding is that most citizens cannot distinguish Nyumba Kumi from
community policing at all
([Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/eastleigh/153827/why-most-eastleigh-residents-just-like-rest-of-kenyans-cant-distinguish-nyumba-kumi-from-community-policing)).

**It is widely read as surveillance.** Scholarship describes it as surveillance of the citizenry and a
state instrument, noting people of other ethnicity or political affiliation than the ruling party are
"particularly sceptical," seeing it as an instrument for intelligence gathering
([Kioko, *Africa Spectrum* 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/000203971705200101);
[open copy](https://www.academia.edu/120158065/Conflict_Resolution_and_Crime_Surveillance_in_Kenya_Local_Peace_Committees_and_Nyumba_Kumi)).
Police-reform literature records communities viewing community policing as "surveillance tools rather
than genuine partnership initiatives," and informants noting police exploiting community information
for personal gain
([Police Reform and Community Policing in Kenya](https://www.academia.edu/73316196/Police_Reform_and_Community_Policing_in_Kenya_The_Bumpy_Road_from_Policy_to_Practice)).
The Mombasa model required a host to introduce visitors to the *balozi* with "full names, gender,
reason for visit, duration of stay, and origin"
([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/life-and-style/dn2/how-nyumba-kumi-could-solve-the-issue-of-national-security-905048)).
That is the exact texture this product must not acquire.

**Four lessons.**
1. **Awareness is not adoption.** 86% knew; it still did not work. A big Facebook launch buys the
   86%, not the participation.
2. **A ten-household unit with a titled local reporter already exists in Kenyan minds, and the
   opinions are bad.** "Block rep" plus "report what you see" is the nyumba kumi silhouette. Use
   *balozi*, or anything shaped like it, and you inherit the whole reputation.
3. **Where community policing is credited, the frame is mutual protection against an outside
   threat** — 82.25% in one northern Kenya study said security would deteriorate without such
   structures ([GCERF](https://www.gcerf.org/community-security-relations-in-kenya/)). The analogue
   here is *protecting each other from fake agents and viewing fees*, not *reporting what is in your
   building.* Same data, opposite story. Pick that one.
4. **Vigilantism is the documented failure mode** of volunteer local enforcement. Anything that lets
   a block rep adjudicate a neighbour will be used that way.

## 5. Who gets left out or exposed

**Ethnicity.** The strongest evidence is economic: in Kibera, when the local chief and landlord share
ethnicity, tenants pay rents **6–11% higher**
([VoxDev on Marx et al.](https://voxdev.org/topic/infrastructure/law-land-ethnic-patronage-kenyas-slums)).
Refusals are documented anecdotally: the Urban Tenants Association of Kenya's Ephraim Murigo says
"instances of landlords putting tribal boundaries are common," with Parklands, South C and Westlands
landlords reported to favour tenants of Asian descent and men from certain communities suffering most
([The Standard / Nairobian](https://standardmedia.co.ke/entertainment/thenairobian/article/2000197016/how-rogue-nairobi-landlords-discriminate-against-tenants-based-on-race-tribe-and-marital-status);
[BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/kenya-tenants-association-says-landlords-discriminate-against-potential-tenants-based-on-ethnicity-race-marital-status/)).
The Standard's own account opens with Joshua Otieno losing a Pangani tenancy *at signing* when the
landlady reacted to his Luo surname
([The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/m/article/2001231312/looking-for-a-house-in-nairobi-your-last-name-could-lock-you-out-as-tenancy-discrimination-by-landlords-spreads)).

Note the stage: *at signing*, not at search. **This is the key product finding.** Removing the
gatekeeper from the search does not remove the filter; it relocates it to the phone call and the gate,
where it is applied to a surname and an accent. Transparency does not reduce discrimination — it makes
it cheaper to apply and more wasteful to experience, because the excluded tenant now pays fare to be
refused in person. The mitigation is not a policy page; it is never publishing a seeker's name before
the viewing.

**Gender and marital status.** Documented: UTAK describes buildings let only to unmarried or only to
married people, with singles locked out past 10pm — "in such if you are single they will not open the
gates to you past 10pm"
([The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/real-estate/article/2000108482/are-you-single-married-what-ethnicity-are-some-of-the-landlord-prejudices-facing-tenants));
a Nairobian report describes Landi Mawe landlords posting notices that only families will be
considered ([Newsday](https://newsday.co.ke/nairobi-estate-landi-mawe-locks-out-single-ladies-prefering-family-ladies/)).
Nation's gender desk documents a landlord using "routine checks" to enter a tenant's house and demand
a sexual favour
([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/news/gender/in-his-routine-checks-my-landlord-entered-my-house-asked-for-sexual-favour--4609520));
SDI documents the same currency at caretaker level. The Pipeline study finds women confined to small
apartments and constrained from responding aggressively "lest they risk being branded immoral"
([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/high-rise-living-in-nairobis-pipeline-estate-is-stressful-how-men-and-women-cope-265499)).
*Reasoning:* a public block-rep title is therefore **differentially dangerous for women**. A named
woman, in a named building, publicly in contact with strangers wanting to see houses, is exposed both
to the caretaker whose favour economy she disrupted and to the moral-reputation sanction the same
research documents. Publish the building, not the resident's name beside it.

**Religion, sexuality, disability.** Documented: an APHRC 2024 study found narratives of eviction or
being barred from renting in **every study site**, with only 26.6% of respondents never having faced
discrimination
([APHRC](https://aphrc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Examination-of-sexual-and-gender-minorities-lived-experiences-in-Kenya.pdf));
HRW records an interviewee evicted from roughly ten houses, with police refusing to intervene with
landlords ([HRW 2015](https://www.hrw.org/report/2015/09/28/issue-violence/attacks-lgbt-people-kenyas-coast));
a landlord ordered 76 LGBT refugees out of Nairobi housing
([OWP](https://theowp.org/lgbt-refugees-eviction-from-safe-housing-in-kenya-a-result-of-discrimination/)).
On disability, Nairobi work finds disabled residents concentrated in the densest, least accessible
parts of the city
([AT2030](https://at2030.org/static/at2030_core/outputs/Inclusive_infrastructure_case_study_Kenya.pdf)),
and the 2023/24 Kenya Housing Survey carries accessibility data by disability status
([KNBS](https://www.knbs.or.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/2023-24-Kenya-Housing-Survey-Basic-Report.pdf)).

*Reasoning:* for all three groups the asymmetry runs the same way, and it is the one genuine social
good here — **a factual field does what a conversation cannot.** Floor number, lift or no lift,
ground-floor availability, gate hours, water days, whether the block has children. Those spare a
wheelchair user, a single mother and a gay tenant the fare and the doorstep refusal. That is a
stronger reason for this product to exist than disintermediation. But a *resident-identity* layer cuts
the other way: a public roster of who lives where, in a country that criminalises same-sex conduct,
is a hazard. Make buildings public, never residence.

## 6. What already exists socially

**The behaviour exists; the product is late, not early.** Kenyan reporting documents renters already
running this: frustration with agency fees pushing tenants into WhatsApp, Facebook and Telegram groups
where members share listings and invite each other to view, with landlords joining to post vacancies
and "eliminating the need for middlemen"
([Bizna Kenya](https://biznakenya.com/social-media-house-hunting-communities-in-kenya/);
[Mwakilishi](https://mwakilishi.com/article/real-estate-news/2025-03-05/whatsapp-and-facebook-become-lifelines-for-house-hunters-in)).
The observable etiquette is a free design correction: **if a house does not suit you, you post it for
the next person.** The actor is the *seeker who has already viewed*, not the resident — and that is
the person with the lowest social cost for publishing a price.

Commercial attempts have converged on the same gap: CribHunter charging a flat KSh 600 by M-Pesa to
unlock landlord contact and location, Spot A Crib running paid house-hunters, Kejanova advertising
escrow-protected viewings. *Reasoning:* "free" therefore enters a market where every adjacent product
charges, which makes free the suspicious option rather than the attractive one.

Fakes are the known pathology: listings priced slightly below market with stolen photos, urgency
pressure, and a small upfront viewing or booking fee — with the honest note that these platforms "do
NOT verify who is posting houses"
([Renters Hub](https://www.rentershub.co.ke/blogs/how-fake-rental-agents-in-nairobi-actually-operate-a-step-by-step-breakdown-of-the-scam-system/)).
*Reasoning on sanction:* nothing formal happens to a faker — screenshotted, abused in the comments,
removed by the admin, back under a new profile the same week. Which is why members rely on the admin's
*personal* reputation, not the group's rules.

**What someone says today when a relative asks "unajua nyumba?"** They do not send a link. They say
*"ngoja nikuulize"* — let me ask — then ring their own caretaker, a friend's caretaker, or the mama
mboga at the gate who knows which plot has a notice up. Then they call back with one house, one phone
number, and their own name attached to the introduction. The existing practice is **a single trusted
relay, not a list.** Any product worse than one phone call to a cousin loses to the cousin.

## 7. The words to use and avoid

*Standard Kiswahili is verifiable; Sheng moves fast and should be confirmed with native speakers in
each target estate before launch — the dictionaries themselves warn the vocabulary changes rapidly
([Go Sheng](https://www.sheng.co.ke/kamusi/?s_word=)).*

| Concept | Use | Avoid, and why |
|---|---|---|
| Vacant unit | **"vacant"** (the actual market word, used by Kenyan sites themselves), **"keja iko"**, "kuna nyumba" | "available unit", "inventory", "property" |
| To report | **"weka hapa"** (put it here), **"ambia watu"** (tell people), **"tuma"** | **"ripoti" / "report"** — the police register and nyumba kumi. Fatal |
| To confirm | **"nimeona"** (I've seen it), **"iko bado"** (it's still there), **"ni kweli"** | **"thibitisha"** (officialese), "verify", "validate", "audit" |
| Caretaker | **"caretaker"** — what everyone actually says | "mlinzi" is the *guard*, often a different man; using it signals you don't know the building. Also "agent", "property manager" |
| Local role | **"mwenyeji wa GG House"** (the local of GG House), or **no title at all** | **"balozi"** — the nyumba kumi title. Also "block rep", "ambassador", "champion", "monitor", "volunteer", "member" |
| Trustworthy person | **"mtu wa maana"**, **"mtu wa kuaminika"**, **"si mtu wa mchezo"**, **"ana jina"** (he has a name) | "trusted partner", "verified user", "certified" |
| It's free | **"hakuna viewing fee"**, **"hatulipishi"** (we don't charge), **"hakuna pesa inahusika"** | **"bure"** alone — it also carries "for nothing / worthless" and reads as bait. "Free!" with an exclamation mark is scam register |

**English words that kill trust, and the signal they send.** *Scam:* "agent", "booking fee",
"commitment fee", "unlock", "limited slots", any link shortener, any "forwarded many times" header.
*NGO:* "community-driven", "empower", "beneficiary", "stakeholder", "inclusive", "capacity building",
"onboarding", "pilot". *Government:* "register", "registration", "data", "survey", "monitoring",
"compliance", "ten households", "report", "officer". The documented Kenyan fact-check pattern is
exactly this combination — fake pages impersonating government and Fuliza while offering something
free ([Africa Check](https://allafrica.com/stories/202504100065.html);
[Fuliza impersonation](https://allafrica.com/stories/202503310352.html)). Institutional register plus
"free" is the scam fingerprint.

**The register that earns trust in a WhatsApp group.** One named human, not a brand. A Safaricom
number that answers. A specific named building and estate in the first line, because specificity is
the only cheap credibility signal. Kiswahili or Sheng sentence frames with English nouns inside, which
is how people actually write. Lowercase, no logo, no banner graphic. A voice note beats a paragraph.
And the move that works here is **anchoring against the known rip-off, not promising a benefit**: not
"free housing platform for Kenyans" but *"hakuna viewing fee. Hii ni list ya nyumba vacant Pipeline.
Nambari ya caretaker iko hapa."*

## What a Kenyan would say about this plan in one sentence

> "Nzuri sana — but mimi niandike kwa list ati nalipa 14k na jirani yangu analipa 16k, na caretaker
> aone jina yangu? Hapana, mimi naambia cousin yangu kwa simu."

*("Very nice — but me, write on a list that I pay 14k while my neighbour pays 16k, and the caretaker
sees my name? No. I'll just tell my cousin on the phone.")*
