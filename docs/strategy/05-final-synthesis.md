# Final Synthesis — Ten Reports

*Five strategy agents attacked the plan. Five research agents went looking for real voices. This is
what all ten add up to, including where they overturned each other.*

Date: 2026-10-19

Evidence base: ~3,290 Google Play reviews, 2,165 YouTube comments from 70 Kenyan videos, 1,425
TikTok comments, 128 X posts, 313 Wazua forum posts, 101 quoted forum/news passages, 70 sources in
the historical study. Plus one complete null result: Facebook could not be read at all.

---

## The reversal you need first

Two messages ago I told you eight Kenyan products were already running your exact pitch, and that
this meant the idea was not scarce.

**That was wrong, and the app data shows why.** Those eight have websites, marketing copy and
pricing pages. They do not have users.

| Product | Reality |
|---|---|
| **BuyRentKenya** — best-known property brand in Kenya | **No app at all.** Not on Play, not on Apple |
| StayNai, CribSeek, HaoFinder, SpaceYako, PropTraka, 254rent, RentU | **No app at all** |
| Property24 Kenya | 50,000+ installs — and its 1★ pile is mostly *"it doesn't open"* |
| Kejanova | 5,000+ installs, **12 reviews** |
| Reemio | 10,000+ installs, **35 reviews** |
| Househunt Kenya | 1,000+ installs, **3.4★** |
| Renters Hub | 500+ installs |
| Nyumba Search | 50+ installs |
| Rentor | 10+ installs |
| **The entire Kenyan rental category on iOS** | **Two user ratings. Total.** |

The category is not crowded. **It is a graveyard of landing pages.** Every one of those companies
worked out the right pitch and then failed to get anybody to use the thing.

So "someone already built this" was the wrong lesson. The right one is harder: **building it is not
the hard part, and neither is the idea.**

## Your real competitor is WhatsApp, and it is winning

Not the portals. Not the apps.

- **Esther Njeri's community: ~21,000 members.** Free. Born from a viral TikTok in early 2025 after
  she lost KSh 2,500 to an agency whose listings did not match what they showed her.
- **"Keja Hunters": one main group plus a community of 54 others** — covering South B and C, Rongai,
  Fedha, Syokimau, Kitengela, Kasarani, Umoja, Kahawa Sukari, Kahawa West, Wendani. **Your target
  estates, by name.**
- **YouTube comment sections are already functioning as the marketplace.** Under one three-year-old
  Kitengela house tour, **36 of 86 comments** are *"Ziko wapi?"* or *"Is it available still"*.
  Twenty-five more under a four-year-old Tassia tour. People post their budget by estate — *"Can I
  get a house around pipeline tassia budget 6k please"* — offer up their own blocks, offer to guide
  strangers *"for free"*, and ask for *"a one bedroom agent free"*.

Free, working, unmoderated, and already in your estates. **Nobody needs persuading to do this. They
are doing it in the wrong places because there is nowhere right to do it.**

## The engine is real, and better than the one I designed

Two norms are already running in those groups, unprompted:

1. **Post the house you are vacating.**
2. **Post the house you viewed and rejected.**

Read the second one again. **It means listing supply scales with failed searches.** Every
disappointed house hunter produces inventory. That is a better engine than anything in my earlier
documents, and it already exists. Copy both norms word for word; do not improve them.

## What is dead: getting the data from the supply side

Four of my five strategy agents independently concluded *recruit the caretaker* — the QuintoAndar
doorman playbook. The evidence kills it, and the way it dies is worth understanding.

**The caretaker defrauds the landlord too.** A Wazua landlord, 2016: *"starting with thieving
agents — They lie to you that the rooms are vacant, yet someone is paying them a lower figure
directly."* A Kenyatalk poster describes a caretaker who *"has been 'eating' rent for 3 months"*
while telling the landlord the unit was empty.

> **The landlord cannot tell you what is vacant in his own building.**

So there is no clean supply-side source. Not the landlord — he is being lied to. Not the caretaker —
the lie is his income, in both directions.

**And there is a Kenyan corpse with the cause of death written on it.** Kejahunt, 2013, listing
houses *"from as low as Sh5,000"* — your exact market. It pivoted away within ten months. The
Standard's own words on why: *"**Despite some caretakers' reluctance to offering information on
vacant houses**, Mutua insists that theirs is a growing business."*

Of course he was reluctant. The information is his income.

**This also retracts my last message.** I told you the product was the caretaker's phone number. The
pin and the date survive. **The number does not** — because the man whose number it is has a
documented, two-sided financial interest in that number staying private, and the last company to
ask him for it died.

What is left: **crowdsourcing from demand.** Which is exactly what the 21,000-member group is
already doing.

## So the only honest question: what does software add to WhatsApp?

Four things. Each is now backed by evidence rather than argument.

### 1. A date. This is the moat, and nobody has ever held it.

- Property24, **2016**: *"The site is never updated with new houses for rent! Its been three months
  now and same old same old!"*
- Property24, **2022**: *"Old adverts... They have 2020 or 2021 dates."*
- **Jiji's single most-upvoted review out of 57,800 — 2,474 people marked it helpful — is about
  year-old listings staying live.**
- **Jiji conceded it in a developer reply:** *"Listings are posted by independent sellers, and
  availability may change quickly. We encourage sellers to keep ads up to date."*
- A Nakuru YouTube channel's workaround is to type **"(BOOKED)"** into its own video title.
- Someone asked *"still available?"* on a **January 2024** TikTok listing in **October 2026** —
  thirty-three months later. 153 of 1,425 comments contained an availability word.

**A ten-year, three-country, unfixed defect, admitted by the market leader.** It is not a technology
problem. It is discipline, and discipline is the one thing a competitor funded by listing volume
cannot copy.

> **The product is not a list of houses. It is a date.**

### 2. Search by price and estate.

WhatsApp cannot do it. Comment sections cannot do it. *"Can I get a house around pipeline tassia
budget 6k please"* is a database query typed into a YouTube comment, because there is nowhere else
to type it. And people think in interchangeable clusters — *"Kasarani ama Roysambu ama Githurai"* —
so it must take several estates at once.

### 3. The all-in cost.

The real number runs **about 40% above advertised rent.** Service charge alone is 13% (a 12,000
house costs 17,000). One advertised at **55k was quoted 130k on arrival.** Ondieki only learned a
Buruburu house's true cost — down to a cockroach-insecticide line item — because **a neighbour told
him.**

The single best artifact in the whole haul is an agent's own advert, @kayhomes, Ruai:

> *Rent 6k · Deposit 6k · Water 200 per unit · Garbage 200 per month · NB: VIEWING FEE APPLICABLE
> (2k)*

**KSh 14,000 to move into a KSh 6,000 house.** Lead with that. It needs no argument.

### 4. Agent-proofing.

Njeri's first group was infiltrated by agents. She shut it down, re-architected into small
area-specific groups, and was then *"threatened with a legal suit by someone I believe is a rogue
agent."* My red team predicted both, in that order. **WhatsApp has no defence against this.
Software can have one.**

## Your price decision was right, and the evidence kept confirming it

You overrode me on showing prices. Three independent findings say you were right:

- **Nobody, anywhere in ten reports, said they would not post a rent publicly.** The researcher who
  looked for it specifically could not find one instance.
- A Pipeline resident published photos of his building's filth **with his rent attached** — on 18
  October 2026, the day before these reports.
- A Ruaka building's WhatsApp group *"ganged up to demand their refunds."*

The caveat the researcher raised honestly: these sources skew towards people who already chose to
speak publicly. But the reticence I warned you about is not visible in the data, and the one real
risk — the landlord levelling everyone up at renewal — is handled by publishing asking prices only.

## What is still genuinely unknown

**1. The caretaker finder's fee: unresolved, leaning plausible.** Nobody said *"I paid the caretaker
X to show me a vacant house."* But @Xoggaar in Eastleigh paid rent and deposit *"plus an amount I
never heard of, which is taken by the person who shows you"* — large enough that his family will not
move again. Four renters under one Thika Road video asked for the caretaker's phone number
directly — *"Not sure if I should call the caretaker or ask you directly, kuna vacant kwa sasa?"* —
so he is plainly the renter's default route to vacancy information. Two caretaker money rackets are
confirmed (cooked water meter readings; tapping the building's power onto a tenant's line). And
Renters Hub's own copy lists its advertisers as *"Landlords, caretakers, and agents"*, assuming
nobody would blink.

So: **he is an unsupervised gatekeeper who already monetises his position. The specific fee is
unproven.**

**2. Facebook is entirely unverified.** Not one member count, admin name or fee practice could be
confirmed from outside. Your main channel is a black box. One logged-in account and two hours fixes
that.

**3. Mombasa, Nakuru, Eldoret and Kisumu are barely covered** by any of the ten reports.

## Three things to do next, in order

**1. Join the competitor. This week.**
Njeri's community and Keja Hunters. Do not pitch anything. Watch. How fresh are the posts? How do
they handle fakes? Does anyone date a listing? How badly are agents in there? That is your
competitor, your possible distribution, and your product spec, in one afternoon.

**2. Open the Google Maps corpus. This is the cheapest unexploited research anywhere.**
Real tenants review their own buildings, and nobody has read it: **Tumaini Estate Apartments in
Pipeline has 744 reviews.** Greenspan in Donholm, 365. About 2,200 across six Syokimau complexes.
And for the caretaker question specifically: **Summerrose Apartments, a rental agency in Embakasi,
3.6★ with 39 reviews**; Kayole_kona 3.9★/57; Gracious Apartments Kasarani 3.9★/22.

The Google Places API returns 5 reviews per place for free. Forty places is roughly **200 real
tenant reviews for almost nothing** — and it is the most likely place the caretaker question gets
settled. Place IDs are already saved from the research run.

**3. Then ask five caretakers one question.** Not about fees — about access:
*"Who decides who gets to walk in and see a vacant unit?"*
The fee question is contaminated; the access question is not, and access is what the evidence
actually shows him controlling.

## The one-line version

> **Don't build a vacancy platform. Build the index over the conversation that is already happening
> in WhatsApp groups and comment sections — and make the date the product, because in ten years and
> three countries nobody has.**

Everything else — the building as the unit, residents confirming, prices on every listing, the
all-in cost, never touching money — fits behind that sentence. The ask does not change either,
because it is already a norm: **post the house you saw and did not take.** Add a date to it.
