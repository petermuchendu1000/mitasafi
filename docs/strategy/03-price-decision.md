# Decision: Every Listing Shows a Price

Date: 2026-10-19
Status: **Decided by the founder. Not open.**

---

## The decision

**Every listing shows a price.** A vacant house with no price is not a listing — it is a rumour. Price
is the first thing a seeker filters on, and without it they cannot decide whether to spend KSh 200
on fare. Any design that hides the price has failed at the one job.

This overrides anything in `02-what-the-agents-found.md` that reads as "no prices."

---

## What the research actually said, correctly stated

The social research did **not** say "do not publish prices." It said something narrower:

> Do not make the **sitting resident** the **source** of the price.

That is a sourcing problem, not a display problem. The two are easy to confuse and they have
completely different answers. Here is the real constraint:

**Show every price. Protect whoever supplied it.**

Both are achievable at once, because the dangerous number and the useful number are not the same
number.

---

## The two different numbers

This is the whole resolution.

| | **Asking price** of the empty unit | **Sitting rent** of occupied units |
|---|---|---|
| Who needs it | The seeker, to decide whether to travel | Nobody, really |
| Who already knows it | Everyone — the caretaker quotes it to every stranger who calls | Only that tenant |
| Can it be traced to one person? | **No.** Anyone who called could have supplied it | **Yes.** Only a handful of people know it |
| Does publishing it start a fight? | No | **Yes** — 2A learns she pays 16k and 3B pays 14k |
| Does it trigger a renewal hike? | No | **Yes** — the landlord reads it too |

So:

> **Publish asking prices freely. Never publish what a sitting tenant pays, per unit.**

The seeker gets everything they need. Both of the explosions the research found came from sitting
rents, not from asking prices. The asking price is also **naturally unattributable** — the caretaker
quotes it to every caller, so he cannot work out who passed it on. That unattributability is the
protection, and it comes free.

---

## Where the price comes from, in order of preference

Four safe sources sit ahead of the risky one. The earlier design's mistake was reaching for number
five first.

**1. The sign itself.** Many Kenyan TO LET signs state the price. If it is painted on the wall, the
landlord published it. Repeating it costs nobody anything. Safest price in the system, and free.

**2. A seeker who already viewed.** They were quoted a price and walked away. No ongoing
relationship to protect, no deposit held hostage. The social research found Kenyans **already doing
this** in WhatsApp and Facebook groups — *"if a house doesn't suit you, you post it for the next
person."* This is the best source for a quoted price, and it is an existing habit rather than a new
one.

**3. The caretaker or landlord.** If he is a declared party who wants the unit filled, he gives the
price himself. He cannot retaliate against himself. **This is where the system should aim** — it is
also the QuintoAndar doorman playbook that four of the five agents converged on.

**4. The person who just moved in.** Knows the *actual* transacted price, including every extra.
Most accurate number available, and zero risk — they have already signed and have no history in the
block to protect. Capture it at move-in confirmation.

**5. A sitting resident.** Last resort only, and only with the protections below.

---

## How to show a price without burning the source

1. **Bands before exact figures.** A single uncorroborated report publishes as *"11,000–13,000"*,
   not *"12,000"*. A band is enough for a seeker to decide whether to travel, and useless for
   working out who leaked. It collapses to an exact figure once the caretaker confirms it or two
   independent sources agree.
2. **Never show how many people confirmed a price.** *"Confirmed by 2 residents"* tells the
   caretaker there are two informers in his block. Show **freshness**, not headcount.
3. **Never publish within hours of a single inside report.** If the price appears the same evening
   someone was seen walking around, he knows who. Batch it.
4. **Drop the source identity internally once fraud scoring is done.** If anyone ever asks you who
   reported a building, you should be unable to answer.
5. **Always publish the all-in cost, not just the rent.** Rent + water + garbage + service charge +
   deposit + advance. The bare asking price is a half-truth and this market's worst habit — the
   hidden-cost problem was the sharpest finding in the whole first analysis. The all-in figure comes
   safely from sources 2 and 4.

---

## The one access rule to keep

From the economics work, and it survives this decision intact because it is about shape, not
content:

- A **seeker** searching *"bedsitter in Kayole under 12k"* sees matching listings with their prices.
  Good. That is the product.
- A **landlord** must not be able to pull *"every asking rent in Kayole, sorted."* That is the
  Danish concrete bulletin, and published prices rose 15–20% when a competition regulator did
  exactly that.

Same data, different access shape. Query by need, never browse by table. It is an access-control
decision, costs nothing, and does not take a single price away from a single seeker.

---

## What this changes in the plan

- Prices appear on every listing. Settled.
- The **first ask of a resident is no longer the price.** It is *"is 3B still empty?"* — one fact,
  safe to give, and the thing that makes the list fresh.
- The **price ask moves to the exit interview**: the seeker who viewed and didn't take it, and the
  seeker who viewed and did. Both are outside the building's power structure.
- The **caretaker becomes the preferred price source**, which is the same conclusion the research
  reached from four other directions. Getting prices from him is one more reason to recruit him
  openly rather than route around him.
