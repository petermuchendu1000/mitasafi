# Voices 01 — Facebook: what Kenyans actually say about house hunting

**Date of research: 2026-10-19**
**Researcher: Claude (automated web research, no Facebook account)**

## READ THIS FIRST — the integrity statement

**I obtained ZERO verbatim quotes from Facebook.** Not one. Facebook is completely
login-walled from this research environment (evidence in section 2). Every quote in
section 3 comes from a source I fetched the raw HTML of and read with my own eyes —
Kenyan newspapers, Kenyan news sites, and Kenyan blogs. Several of those quote
Twitter/X posts verbatim; those are labelled. None quote Facebook posts.

**Real quote count: 49 verbatim quotes in a real person's own words** (the numbered items
in section 3), all with URLs — plus clearly-labelled journalist description, article
text and company copy, which are not people's voices. **Facebook-origin quotes: 0.**

Where I write "my summary" or "company copy", that is not a person's voice and should
not be used as evidence of what renters say.

## 1. The group census

### 1a. What I actually need to say up front

**The founder's distribution plan — "post in large Kenyan Facebook groups" — could not
be verified in any respect.** I could not confirm that a single named rental group
exists today, its size, its rules, whether it is public or private, who admins it, or
whether admins charge. Facebook *groups* are not indexed by any search engine I could
reach and not viewable without login. Facebook *Pages* and *Marketplace* ARE partly
indexed, so the census below is heavily skewed toward Pages — which are a different
distribution channel with different dynamics (one-to-many broadcast by an agent, not
a community).

### 1e. What the numbers add up to

**My summary, not a quote.** They add up to almost nothing you can plan a business on:

- **Two** rental-specific Facebook groups are named anywhere in a citable source, with
  counts that are 7.5 years stale, totalling 365,861 members in May 2019.
- **Zero** verified-today group names, URLs, sizes, rules, admin identities, or
  posting-fee policies.
- **14** Facebook Pages whose names and URLs I can hand over, none of which I could
  read, none of which is a community — they are agent shopfronts. Posting a free
  vacancy list to an agent's Page is posting to a competitor.
- The single largest Facebook surface for Kenyan rentals that is actually *indexed* is
  **Marketplace**, not groups — a Meta-run classifieds product that already does
  roughly what the founder wants to build, in every target town, for free.

**The honest conclusion for a founder about to bet distribution on this: the plan is
currently unverifiable from outside Facebook. It must be verified from inside, with a
logged-in Kenyan account, before any money is spent.** See section 5.

## 2. What I could NOT access — specific and honest

This section is required and I am not softening it.

**Facebook: total failure. Zero content retrieved.**

- `https://www.facebook.com/groups/<slug>` — redirects to
  `https://www.facebook.com/login/?next=...`. Tested with Chrome, Safari and Googlebot
  user-agents; tested on `www.`, `m.` and `mbasic.` hosts. All identical.
- `https://www.facebook.com/rentershub.co.ke/` (a public Page) — login wall.
- `https://www.facebook.com/marketplace/114751268540391/houses-for-rent/` — login wall.
- `https://www.facebook.com/marketplace/item/2426234484436597/` — login wall.
- `https://www.facebook.com/groups/1449743915353503/about` — login wall.
- Via the `r.jina.ai` reader proxy (server-side fetch) — same login wall, identical
  output. Facebook does not serve group or Page content to any unauthenticated fetch.

So: **no member counts, no "Public group" badges, no creation dates, no admin names, no
pinned rules, no post frequency, no post text, no comment threads. None of it.** Every
Facebook fact in section 1 is second-hand.

**Search engines: mostly blocked from this environment.**

- `web.archive.org` — connection reset at TCP level. No Wayback snapshots of Facebook
  group pages, which was my best hope for historical member counts.
- Google — serves a JS-only shell to curl; no results parseable.
- DuckDuckGo (html + lite) — anti-bot challenge (HTTP 202).
- Mojeek — HTTP 403. Startpage — 303 redirect loop. searx.be — captcha.
- Bing — reachable, but geo-resolves to the US and returned Baytown, Texas rental
  listings for a Nairobi query; `site:facebook.com/groups` returned zero group URLs.
  Bing does not index Facebook groups.
- Reddit — `reddit.com/r/Kenya/search.json` returns an HTML wall; via jina, HTTP 403
  "You've been blocked by network security." `old.reddit.com` 302s. **No Reddit content
  at all**, so no r/Kenya house-hunting threads.
- Quora — HTTP 403.
- X / Twitter — not fetchable. The X quotes in section 3 are ones Kenyan outlets
  reproduced in their own articles.
- TikTok — only `/discover` SEO stubs appear in search; no comment threads readable.
- `nation.africa` — HTTP 403 to direct fetch. **Workaround that did work:** Nation
  Media mirrors the same articles on `mwananchi.co.tz` and `mwanaspoti.co.tz` at the
  same path, and those serve full text. Several key sources below use mirror URLs for
  that reason; the canonical nation.africa URL is also given.
- `kenyatalk.com` (Kenyan forum, likely rich in this material) — HTTP 403.

**One more limitation worth flagging:** the managed fetch tool refuses to reproduce
article text verbatim on copyright grounds. Every quote below was therefore taken from
raw HTML I downloaded and read directly, which is why the source list is narrower than
the number of pages I found but the quotes are exact.

### Theme A — Viewing fees and agent fees, in the renter's own words
*Source: "House hunters ditch expensive agents for WhatsApp groups", by Ndugu Abisai,
Business Daily / Nation Media Group, Wednesday 19 March 2025. Read at the Nation Media
mirror: <https://www.mwanaspoti.co.tz/bd/lifestyle/design-interiors/house-hunters-ditch-expensive-agents-for-whatsapp-groups-4971700>*
*Origin: newspaper interview. Not Facebook.*

1. Derrick Momanyi, 25, software developer, Nairobi:
   > "Looking for a house is hectic. I spent the first two months of the year physically visiting places I liked with no success,"

2. Momanyi, on the fee structure — **the single most important quote in this document**:
   > "I found an agent who I think was out to make a killing from me. His "appearance fee" is Sh2,000. This is money he charges for information—fair enough. If you want him to take you physically to the mentioned house, he charges an additional Sh1,500, which he refers to as an inconvenience fee."
   Context: two separate fees — one to be *told* about a house, one to be *shown* it.
   The article notes this totals Sh3,500 for one house.

3. Momanyi, on why agents waste your time deliberately:
   > "I believe most of these agents have gone rogue. I'd have no problem paying for a service that is fairly charged. But, the agents deliberately take you to a house pulled so far away from your outlined specifications for them to conveniently offer to take you to another one that is closer to what you want."

4. Momanyi, on rent as a trap:
   > "You don't realise these things until you start paying rent. Some of the quoted amounts would convert me to a rent-paying slave. I'd only be working to pay rent."

5. Momanyi, on what should be basic:
   > "I wanted a spacious place with at least one balcony, not too far from a main road, and with reliable amenities like water, power, and internet connectivity. You'd think that's the norm and not the exception when looking for housing; these shouldn't be presented as extras or benefits that come with an apartment."

6. Esther Njeri, 28, moving from Naivasha to Nairobi — the bait-and-switch mechanic:
   > "I was coming from a place of desperation and was pressed for time to move. When I paid the viewing and inconvenience fees, all the houses the agent had shared on Whatsapp suddenly became occupied."

7. Njeri:
   > "I was completely sold out on a chat with an agent with very nice-looking ready-for-occupation apartments."

8. Wanjiru Kariuki, 31, social and community manager:
   > "I was not a follower of Ms Njeri, she just appeared on my for you page (FYP) lamenting about a housing problem I resonated with. I have lost money in house hunting. I paid Sh3,650 for a housing agency to find me a house. I feel like I got played because they kept taking me in circles until one month lapsed, and I was asked to pay more money—think of this as a subscription fee. So, when I saw Ms Njeri's video, I picked it up and made a video on the same, which went viral,"

9. Kariuki, on double-dipping — **the landlord had already paid the agent**:
   > "I had the opportunity to meet a landlord whose property I had been shown by an agent, when I interrogated about the fees I was supposed to pay, the landlord told me that they had already paid the agent for directing potential clients to the property. This means they wanted double pay for the work."

10. Ribin Ondwari, real estate legal expert:
    > "some of these existing regulations were enacted as far back as the 50s. The market has moved way beyond the law, and there is a need for them to be updated. The other reason is that there are individuals who work outside The Estate Agents Act, which means they do not have a code of conduct to adhere to."

## 5. What surprised me — and what contradicts the plan

**All of this is my analysis, clearly labelled as mine, built only on the quotes above.**

**1. The distribution plan rests on an unexaminable asset.** The founder's entire
go-to-market is "post in large Kenyan Facebook groups", and those groups cannot be seen,
sized, counted, or rule-checked from outside Facebook at all. Not by me, not by Google,
not by Bing, not by the Wayback Machine. The only citable member counts in existence are
from May 2019. A strategist would assume group sizes are a researchable input. They are
not. **Before anything else, someone with a Kenyan Facebook account must spend one
afternoon inside Facebook search listing the real groups, their real member counts,
their public/private status, and photographing their pinned rules.** That is a half-day
task that de-risks the whole plan, and it has not been done.

**2. The fight already moved to WhatsApp, Telegram and TikTok — and it was won there in
2025.** Every single primary account of people successfully organising around agents
names WhatsApp, Telegram, TikTok or X. Facebook appears exactly once in a first-person
account (quote 24, from 2018) and the verdict there is damning in a specific way: the
*leads were good*, the *phone numbers were dead or fraudulent*. Esther Njeri's community
reached ~21,000 members on WhatsApp, originated from a TikTok video, and is explicitly
free ("This is a free service"). Momanyi found his actual house via **Telegram**. If the
founder's thesis is "mass-market Kenyans coordinate housing on free social channels",
the evidence supports it strongly — and points at WhatsApp/TikTok, not Facebook.

**3. The viewing fee is unbundled into two fees, and that is the real wedge.** I expected
one fee. Momanyi describes *Sh2,000 to be told about a house* and *Sh1,500 more to be
shown it* (quote 2). The information itself is the priced good. A free vacancy list does
not compete on the Sh1,500 "take me there" leg; it destroys the Sh2,000 "tell me where"
leg. That is a sharper and smaller value proposition than "replace the agent", and it is
the one the evidence actually supports.

**4. Nobody in any source I read tips a caretaker. The hypothesis is live but unsupported.**
I looked hard. The caretaker turns up as the *seller* of access (demanding ~Sh2,500 —
quote 58), as a suspected conman (quote 20), as the person who inflated a water bill
(quote 39), and as the person who vanishes when you want your deposit (quote 40). Only
competitor marketing copy frames the caretaker as "the person who knows what's vacant"
(quotes 61–64), and it frames that as drudgery to be eliminated, not as a relationship
to be cultivated. **A strategist would assume the caretaker is a cheap, friendly supply
channel. The readable evidence says the caretaker is a toll booth.** That changes
supply-side acquisition entirely: caretakers may be competitors for the Sh2,500, not
allies — and they may need to be paid, not merely asked.

**5. Vacancies in the target band do not sit around waiting to be listed.** "An
unoccupied house can attract up to 10 viewers in a day" (quote 15, Kisumu 2014). Victor
Yassin was *outbid after payment* by someone paying seven months upfront (quote 45). A
free list of vacant houses has a data-freshness problem measured in hours, not days. The
listing-site failure mode Wanjiku Miana describes — "about 20 houses in far flung
estates" (quote 27) — is exactly what a stale free list becomes. Freshness, not
coverage, is the product.

**6. Stating your rent publicly is completely normal — the awkwardness is about dignity,
not price.** "Emily" gave exact Kilimani figures to a TV crew. @C_Roiginson posted a
KSh 680,000 unit price and a KSh 3,890 monthly payment. @RedSabin named 10k Ruaka and
25k Jamhuri. Nobody hedged. What people *did* fight about was whether a bedsitter is
dignified for a mother (quotes 48–55). **A product should ask for rent amounts freely,
and be careful about anything that implies a judgement on the kind of house someone
lives in.**

**7. The money lost per person is large relative to the rent.** Paul Wambua lost
Sh22,500 — roughly 2 months' rent in the target band. Millicent Auma burned Sh4,000 on
viewing fees alone, around one month's rent in Pipeline. For a 4,000–30,000/month renter
these are move-killing sums, and they are spent *before* seeing a house. That is the
cash wall, and it is made of search costs, not just deposit + advance.

**8. The free-sharing etiquette is real but I have only a journalist's word for it.**
Bizna Kenya's reporter says he went into these groups and that members post houses that
didn't suit them for others (quote 56), and that landlords have joined and post
vacancies directly (quote 57). **This is the single most important behavioural claim for
the product and I could not verify it with one member's own words.** It needs a
logged-in Kenyan researcher to confirm or kill.

**9. Two Kenyan competitors are already live in the exact niche, one of them six weeks
old.** Keja Chap Chap/HouseHunt Kenya explicitly targets "scam-free bedsitters, studio
rooms, 1-bedroom, and 2-bedroom apartments under KES 30,000" (Sep 2026). Hao Finder
charges KES 5,000 flat under 30K rent and states "Hao Finder Services are NOT Free."
Wanjiru Kariuki was building a landlord-tenant platform in March 2025. Esther Njeri runs
a free 21,000-member community. **"Nobody is doing this" is false. Several people are,
and the free one has 21,000 members and came from TikTok.**

**10. Facebook Marketplace is the incumbent, and it is free.** The only Facebook surface
I could find indexed for Kenyan rentals is Marketplace, which already has a dedicated
"Houses for Rent" hub for Nairobi, Mombasa, Nakuru, Eldoret, Kisumu, Thika, Ruiru, Ngong,
Kikuyu and ~20 smaller towns (section 1d). A strategist would frame the competitor as
"agents". The readable evidence says the incumbent free vacancy list is Meta's.

## Recommended next step (my view, not a finding)

This document is as far as outside-Facebook research can go. The next unit of work is
not more desk research — it is **one logged-in Kenyan Facebook account and two hours**,
producing: real group names, real member counts, public/private status, screenshots of
